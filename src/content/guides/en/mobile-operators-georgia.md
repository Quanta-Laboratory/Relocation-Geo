Georgia has **three mobile network operators**, and for most new arrivals any of them will do the essential job — cheap data, good city coverage and eSIM — within minutes of landing. Where they differ is in coverage reach, whether they also sell you home internet, and the small conveniences like an airport SIM desk. This guide compares **Magti, Silknet and Cellfie** and explains how to choose, buy and register, checked against **official sources**.

For the broader picture — data pricing, home fibre, and why a local number gates banking and government services — see **[mobile and internet in Georgia](/en/mobile-and-internet-in-georgia)**. This page focuses on the operators themselves.

## The three operators at a glance

| Operator | Also known as | 4G / 5G | eSIM | Home internet | Notable for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Magti** | Magticom | Yes / Yes | Yes | Yes (fibre) | Widest coverage; airport tourist SIM |
| **Silknet** | mobile brand **Geocell** | Yes / Yes | Yes | Yes (fibre, DSL, wireless) | Bundling mobile with TV and home internet |
| **Cellfie** | Cellfie Mobile (ex-**Beeline**) | Yes / Yes | Yes (~1 GEL) | No — mobile only | Simple mobile-only plans, cheap eSIM |

All three run their own networks and are regulated by the **Georgian National Communications Commission (ComCom)**. Retail plans and promotions change often, so treat any specific bundle you read about as a snapshot and check the operator's current offer when you buy.

## Magti (Magticom)

**Magti** is the operator most associated with the **widest coverage**, and it markets the **largest 5G footprint** in the country. It runs the full range of technologies from 2G through 5G, supports **eSIM** (activated through the MyMagti app, the website or a service centre), and — usefully for arrivals — sells a **Welcome SIM for tourists at Tbilisi and Kutaisi airports**, valid for around 15 days with a data allowance and some free local calls.

Magti also provides **fixed fibre home internet** and IPTV, so it is a natural choice if you want your phone line and your apartment's broadband from one provider. If your priority is service in **smaller towns or along mountain routes**, Magti is the common default — though you should still check its coverage map for the specific areas you will spend time in.

## Silknet (Geocell)

**Silknet** is one of Georgia's leading internet and TV providers, and its **mobile service runs under the Geocell brand** — a name you will still see, since Geocell was a long-established mobile operator before the merger. It offers **4G and 5G**, supports **eSIM** (with an online order flow and a list of compatible devices), and publishes **tourist packages** for visitors.

Silknet's strength is **bundling**: mobile, fixed **fibre and DSL internet**, wireless home internet, and television from a single provider. That makes it attractive if you are furnishing a long-term apartment and want one bill for connectivity and TV. We could not confirm an airport sales desk from Silknet's official site, so plan to buy at a branch or online rather than at arrivals.

## Cellfie (Cellfie Mobile)

**Cellfie Mobile** is the third operator, **formerly Beeline** — its apps still carry the "MyBeeline" heritage. It runs **4G and 5G** with VoLTE, and its **eSIM is the cheapest and simplest to get**: around **1 GEL**, buyable online at any hour, with the same services and packages as a physical SIM.

The key thing to know is that **Cellfie is mobile-only** — it does not sell fixed home internet or TV, unlike Magti and Silknet. That is not a drawback if all you need is a phone line: its straightforward prepaid plans and cheap online eSIM make it a clean choice for a mobile number without the rest. It does not appear to run a dedicated inbound tourist SIM in the way Magti does, so for a first line on arrival Magti's airport pack is the easier route.

## How to buy and register

You can buy a SIM at an **operator branch**, at the **airport** (reliably from Magti), in many **shops and kiosks**, or set up an **eSIM online** before you even land. To register a prepaid SIM you will **normally need your passport** — registering a line to an identified person is standard practice in Georgia, and a foreign national is expected to present a passport at an official point of sale. Activation is usually quick, and you top up online or in shops.

If your phone supports **eSIM**, that is often the smoothest path: you can order a line from the operator's website or app and be connected without visiting a store. Cellfie's low-cost eSIM and Magti's app-based activation are both designed for this.

## Keeping your number if you switch

Georgia has operated **mobile number portability since 2011**, overseen by ComCom, so you are **not locked to your first operator**. If you start on one network and later prefer another's coverage or bundle, you can **port your existing number** between Magti, Silknet and Cellfie using the receiving operator's "port in" process, rather than losing the number and telling everyone a new one. This matters if you have already given a Georgian number to your **bank or the tax portal**, since those are tied to the number, not the operator.

## A note on numbers and regulation

Georgia's international dialling code is **+995**, and **mobile numbers are nine digits beginning with 5** (written as +995 5XX XXX XXX). The sector is overseen by the **[Georgian National Communications Commission (ComCom)](https://comcom.ge/en/)**, while operators set their own retail tariffs.

Because prices, bundle sizes and tourist-package contents change frequently, we deliberately avoid quoting specific tariffs here — check the operator's current offer at the point of purchase. This page describes the market as published by official sources; it is **not an endorsement of any operator**, and you should compare current coverage and plans for your own needs before choosing.
