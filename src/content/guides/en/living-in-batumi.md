Batumi is Georgia's **largest Black Sea city** and the capital of the **Autonomous Republic of Adjara**. It combines seaside living with modern infrastructure, making it one of the country's most popular destinations for both tourists and foreigners relocating to Georgia.

With around **236,000 residents**, Batumi is much smaller than Tbilisi and offers a more relaxed lifestyle. Many people choose it for its mild climate, palm-lined seafront, modern apartment developments and easy access to the beach. This guide introduces the city's neighbourhoods, climate and lifestyle. For practical information about finding accommodation, see our **[Housing in Georgia](/en/housing-in-georgia)** guide, and for typical prices, see **[Cost of Living in Georgia](/en/cost-of-living-in-georgia)**.

## The city at a glance

Batumi lies on the **Black Sea coast**, close to the Turkish border and surrounded by the green foothills of the Lesser Caucasus.

The city blends a charming historic centre with one of Europe's longest seaside promenades — **Batumi Boulevard** — alongside modern skyscrapers, hotels and residential towers built over the past two decades.

Tourism is one of Batumi's main industries, together with hospitality, real estate, shipping and an increasing number of remote workers who choose the city for its coastal lifestyle. During the summer months the population grows significantly as visitors arrive from across Georgia and abroad.

## Climate

Batumi has a **humid subtropical climate**, with warm, humid summers and some of the mildest winters in Georgia.

Unlike Tbilisi, Batumi is known for its **high rainfall** and lush greenery. It is the **wettest city in Georgia**, receiving heavy rainfall throughout the year, particularly in autumn. While summers are warm rather than extremely hot, the high humidity can make temperatures feel higher.

If you enjoy green landscapes, mild winters and living close to the sea, Batumi's climate is one of its biggest attractions.

## Neighbourhoods

Because the city is built along the coast, choices tend to be defined by distance from the sea:

- **Old Town and central boulevard** — the historic core near the seafront, walkable and central, with a mix of older and restored buildings.
- **New Boulevard / seafront** — the newer stretch of coast lined with modern towers and apartments; the most expensive and tourism-oriented area.
- **Residential areas inland** — quieter districts set back from the sea, generally more affordable.
- **Nearby coast** — spots such as **Gonio** and **Makhinjauri**, just outside the city, for those wanting a quieter coastal base.

Properties close to the beach generally command the highest prices, while accommodation further inland is usually more affordable. You can compare rental costs in our  [cost-of-living guide](/en/cost-of-living-in-georgia).

## Transport

Batumi is a compact city, and many daily destinations can be reached on foot or by bicycle.

Public buses and ride-hailing services are inexpensive and widely available. Unlike Tbilisi, Batumi **does not have a metro**, but traffic is generally lighter and travelling across the city takes much less time.

**Batumi International Airport** offers year-round and seasonal flights to destinations in Europe, the Middle East and neighbouring countries. Trains and buses provide regular connections with **Tbilisi**, **Kutaisi** and the rest of Georgia. Batumi is also home to one of Georgia's most important commercial seaports.

## Seasonal lifestyle

Batumi changes significantly throughout the year. Summer brings beaches, festivals, restaurants and a lively atmosphere, while the winter months are quieter and many seasonal businesses reduce their opening hours. Some people enjoy the peaceful off-season, while others prefer the year-round activity of Tbilisi.

## Is Batumi right for you?

Batumi is an excellent choice if you want to live **by the sea**, enjoy a **slower pace of life**, or work remotely while still having access to modern amenities.

Compared with Tbilisi, it offers a more relaxed atmosphere, milder winters and easier access to nature, but it also has a **smaller job market**, fewer international schools and more limited healthcare options. The city is also noticeably more seasonal, becoming much busier during the summer tourist season.

If your priorities are career opportunities, business, higher education or the widest choice of services, **[Tbilisi](/en/living-in-tbilisi)** may be a better fit.

## Safety

Batumi is generally considered a **safe city**, with relatively low levels of violent crime. Most residents and visitors feel comfortable walking around the city centre, the Boulevard and residential neighbourhoods during both the day and the evening.

As in any tourist destination, **pickpocketing and petty theft** can occasionally occur, particularly in busy areas during the summer season, so it is sensible to keep an eye on your belongings.

Overall, Batumi is widely regarded as one of the safer coastal cities in the region and is a comfortable place to live for families, retirees, remote workers and international residents.
