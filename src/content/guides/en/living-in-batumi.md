Batumi is Georgia's principal Black Sea city — a resort and port on the country's south-western coast, and the capital of the **Autonomous Republic of Adjara**. It is much smaller than Tbilisi and shaped by the sea and by tourism, with a modern seafront and a pronounced seasonal rhythm. In the 2024 census it had about **236,000 residents** (Geostat). This guide outlines the city's setting, climate, districts and connections at a high level; for the practical detail of finding and paying for a home, see the [housing](/en/housing-in-georgia) and [cost-of-living](/en/cost-of-living-in-georgia) guides.

## Setting and character

Batumi sits on the Black Sea coast near the Turkish border, backed by the green foothills of the Lesser Caucasus. It combines a historic old town with a long seafront promenade (the "Boulevard") and a newer district of high-rise towers, hotels and apartments built up over the past two decades. As a resort and port city, its economy leans heavily on tourism, hospitality, shipping and, increasingly, remote work — and its population and activity swell in summer and ebb in the off-season.

## Climate

Batumi has a **humid subtropical climate** with mild winters and warm, humid summers. Its defining feature is rain: it is the **wettest city in Georgia**, receiving on the order of 2,500 mm of precipitation a year, spread through the seasons, with autumn the rainiest period. Summers on the coast are warm and muggy rather than extreme, and winters are noticeably milder than inland Tbilisi. Anyone planning to settle here should factor in the heavy rainfall and the strong seasonal swing in activity.

## Neighbourhoods

Because the city is built along the coast, choices tend to be defined by distance from the sea:

- **Old Town and central boulevard** — the historic core near the seafront, walkable and central, with a mix of older and restored buildings.
- **New Boulevard / seafront** — the newer stretch of coast lined with modern towers and apartments; the most expensive and tourism-oriented area.
- **Residential areas inland** — quieter districts set back from the sea, generally more affordable.
- **Nearby coast** — spots such as **Gonio** and **Makhinjauri**, just outside the city, for those wanting a quieter coastal base.

Seafront property commands the highest prices; areas away from the coast are cheaper. Typical rents by area are set out in the [cost-of-living guide](/en/cost-of-living-in-georgia).

## Getting around and connectivity

Batumi is compact and easy to cover on foot, with local buses and ride-hailing apps for longer trips; unlike Tbilisi it has **no metro**. **Batumi International Airport**, just south of the city, handles seasonal and year-round flights to a range of destinations, and road and rail links connect the city to Tbilisi, Kutaisi and the rest of Georgia. Batumi is also one of the country's main **seaports**. Everyday transport fares and typical taxi costs are covered in the [cost-of-living guide](/en/cost-of-living-in-georgia).

## Who Batumi suits

Batumi suits people who want to live by the sea, prefer a smaller city and milder winters, or work in tourism, hospitality or remote roles. It is quieter and more seasonal than the capital, with a narrower range of jobs, schools and healthcare, so those who need the full spread of services and year-round activity may prefer **[Tbilisi](/en/living-in-tbilisi)**.
