If you no longer need your **Individual Entrepreneur (IE)** status in Georgia, you must **formally terminate it** through the relevant state authorities. This guide sets out the steps and the documents involved, whether you complete the process in person or remotely.

Simply stopping activity is **not enough** — your IE remains active in the system until it is formally cancelled. Until then you may still:

- be required to file tax declarations;
- receive penalties from the Revenue Service;
- appear as an active taxpayer.

Termination ensures your obligations are fully closed. See also the guides on [running a business in Georgia](/en/run-a-business-in-georgia) and [registering an Individual Entrepreneur remotely](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely).

## Authorities involved

| Authority | Role |
| --- | --- |
| National Agency of Public Registry | Termination of IE status |
| Revenue Service of Georgia | Tax closure and status updates |

## Terminating remotely

If you are not in Georgia, an IE can be terminated through a **representative located in Georgia** acting under a Power of Attorney. The representative submits the documents to the **National Agency of Public Registry** at the **Public Service Hall** on your behalf.

| Step | Action | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appoint a representative in Georgia | — |
| 2 | Prepare the Power of Attorney (template provided by the representative) | Your country |
| 3 | Notarise the Power of Attorney and a copy of your passport; apostille or legalise if required | Your country |
| 4 | Settle tax obligations: file pending declarations, pay outstanding taxes, cancel special tax status | Revenue Service |
| 5 | Send the original documents to the representative by international courier | Your country → Tbilisi |
| 6 | Translate the documents into Georgian and notarise the translation | Georgia |
| 7 | Submit the documents and terminate the IE | Public Service Hall |

## Power of Attorney

The Power of Attorney authorises your representative to:

- terminate the Individual Entrepreneur;
- submit documents to government agencies on your behalf;
- receive registration extracts and related documents.

You sign it before a **notary in your country**, together with a **notarised copy of the main page of your passport**. Depending on your country, these documents may also need to be **apostilled** (for countries party to the Hague Apostille Convention) or otherwise **legalised**.

## Translation and notarisation in Georgia

Once the originals arrive in Georgia, the representative arranges an **official translation into Georgian** and **notarisation of the translation**. This translated, notarised set is what is submitted to the Public Registry.

## Settling tax obligations

Before termination you should:

- submit all pending declarations;
- pay any outstanding taxes or penalties;
- cancel any special tax status, such as Small Business status, if applicable.

## State fees and processing time

The documents are submitted to the **National Agency of Public Registry** at the **Public Service Hall**. The registry reviews the application and issues a decision on termination.

| Service | State fee | Processing time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | GEL 26 | 1 working day |
| Same-day | GEL 75 | Same day |

## Legal sources

This guide is based on the following legislation of Georgia:

- **Tax Code of Georgia**;
- **Law of Georgia on Entrepreneurs**;
- **Law of Georgia on Public Registry**;
- **Government of Georgia Ordinance No. 509** on the service fees, payment procedures and service timeframes of the National Agency of Public Registry (29 December 2011).

Official versions are published by the **Legislative Herald of Georgia (Matsne)** at [matsne.gov.ge](https://www.matsne.gov.ge/en). Service fees and timeframes are periodically updated, so confirm current requirements with the **Public Service Hall**, the **National Agency of Public Registry** or the **Revenue Service** before applying.
