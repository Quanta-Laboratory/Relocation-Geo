This guide covers bringing **cats, dogs and ferrets** into Georgia as a **non-commercial movement** — that is, pets travelling with their owner and not intended for sale or transfer of ownership. It is general information; confirm current rules before travelling or importing.

The requirements are set by **Government Resolution No 551 of 16 November 2018** on the non-commercial movement of domestic animals across the customs border of Georgia. The **National Food Agency (NFA)** is the competent veterinary authority; the **Revenue Service** carries out checks at the border.

## Which animals this covers

The non-commercial movement rules apply to three species that accompany an owner or authorised person and remain under their responsibility:

- **dogs** (Canis lupus familiaris);
- **cats** (Felis silvestris catus);
- **ferrets / polecats** (Mustela putorius furo).

Other pets — including birds, rodents, hares, reptiles, amphibians and most invertebrates — are handled under separate veterinary provisions and preventive-health measures. If you are moving one of these, confirm the requirement with the National Food Agency before travel.

## Entry requirements for cats and dogs

To move a cat, dog or ferret into Georgia, the animal must meet all of the following conditions.

| Requirement | What is needed |
| --- | --- |
| Identification (microchip) | A microchip (transponder) meeting **ISO standard 11784** with **HDX or FDX-B** technology, readable by an **ISO 11785** reader. A legible tattoo is accepted only if applied **before 1 January 2020**. |
| Rabies vaccination | A **valid rabies vaccination** meeting the validity requirements set in the rules. |
| Rabies antibody test | A **rabies antibody titration (titre) test** meeting the validity requirements. |
| Parasite treatment | Treatment with a **broad-spectrum anthelmintic** against internal and external parasites (endoparasites and exoparasites). |
| Veterinary document | A duly completed **Health Certificate**. An **EU pet passport** or an equivalent identification document from another country may be presented instead. |
| Owner's declaration | A signed **declaration** (Annex No 5 of the Rule) confirming the movement is **non-commercial**. |

The microchip should be implanted and read so that the animal can be reliably matched to its veterinary document. The identification, vaccination and testing must be recorded in the Health Certificate or passport.

Because the exact **timing and validity windows** for rabies vaccination and the antibody test depend on the country of departure and are periodically updated, **confirm the current requirement with the National Food Agency** before you travel. Do not rely on a fixed number of days from unofficial sources.

## What the Health Certificate must contain

The Health Certificate or passport must include, among other details:

- the microchip (or tattoo) **location, code, and date of implantation or reading**;
- the animal's **species, breed, date of birth, sex and colour**;
- the **certificate number**;
- the **owner's name and contact details**;
- the **veterinarian's name, contact details and signature**;
- **rabies vaccination details**;
- the **date of blood sampling** for the rabies antibody titration test;
- confirmation of **preventive health measures** for diseases other than rabies;
- the **certifying and inspecting authority's details and inspection date**.

The signed owner's **declaration** confirming non-commercial purpose is an integral part of the Health Certificate or passport and must be submitted.

## Number of animals

The maximum number of animals in a single non-commercial movement that may accompany one owner or authorised person is **five**.

More than five is permitted only if **all** of the following apply:

- the movement is for **competitions, exhibitions or sports events**, or training related to them;
- the owner submits **written proof** that the animals are registered for such an event or with the organising association;
- the animals are **more than six months old**.

In all other cases, animals above the limit are subject to the general veterinary inspection set by Georgian legislation.

## Entry points and border checks

Pets should enter through a **customs checkpoint** where veterinary control is carried out. The Revenue Service handles the border check and the National Food Agency supervises veterinary compliance. Present the Health Certificate (or passport) and the signed declaration to the officers on arrival.

Because permitted crossing points and on-the-spot procedures can change, confirm the **entry point and any advance-notice requirement** with the Revenue Service or the National Food Agency before travelling.

## Before you travel

- Confirm the **current rabies vaccination and antibody-test timing** with the National Food Agency for your country of departure.
- Make sure the **microchip is ISO-compliant** and its code matches every document.
- Have the **Health Certificate or pet passport** completed and signed by an authorised veterinarian, plus the **non-commercial declaration**.
- Check the **number of animals** you are bringing against the five-animal limit.

This is general information; confirm the current requirement or fee with the **National Food Agency** and the **Revenue Service** before travelling or importing.
