This guide explains the rules for bringing **cats, dogs and ferrets** into Georgia as a **non-commercial movement**—that is, where the animals accompany their owner (or an authorised person), are not intended for sale or transfer of ownership, and remain under the owner's responsibility.

It provides general information only. Before travelling, always confirm the current requirements with the competent Georgian authorities.

The applicable rules are set out in **Government Resolution No. 551 of 16 November 2018** on the **non-commercial movement of pet animals across the customs border of Georgia**. Veterinary requirements are administered by the **National Food Agency (NFA)**, while border controls are carried out by the competent border authorities in accordance with Georgian legislation.

## Which animals this guide covers

The non-commercial movement rules covered by this guide apply to three species:

- **dogs** (_Canis lupus familiaris_);
- **cats** (_Felis silvestris catus_); and
- **ferrets** (_Mustela putorius furo_).

These rules apply where the animal accompanies its owner or an authorised person and remains under that person's responsibility throughout the movement.

Other animals—including birds, rodents, rabbits and hares, reptiles, amphibians and most invertebrates—are subject to different veterinary requirements and import procedures. If you intend to bring another type of pet into Georgia, confirm the applicable requirements directly with the **National Food Agency** before travelling.

## Entry requirements for cats, dogs and ferrets

To bring a cat, dog or ferret into Georgia under the non-commercial movement rules, the animal must satisfy all applicable veterinary and documentary requirements described below.

| Requirement                | What is needed                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identification (microchip) | A microchip (transponder) complying with **ISO Standard 11784** and using **HDX or FDX-B** technology, readable by an **ISO 11785** scanner. A clearly legible tattoo is accepted only if it was applied **before 1 January 2020**. |
| Rabies vaccination         | A **valid rabies vaccination** administered and recorded in accordance with the applicable veterinary requirements.                                                                                                                 |
| Rabies antibody test       | **Where required**, a valid **rabies antibody titration (titre) test** meeting the applicable veterinary requirements. Whether this test is required depends on the country or territory of departure.                              |
| Parasite treatment         | Treatment with a **broad-spectrum anthelmintic** against internal and external parasites (endoparasites and ectoparasites), where required under the applicable rules.                                                              |
| Veterinary document        | A properly completed **Health Certificate**. Where accepted under the applicable rules, an **EU pet passport** or another recognised veterinary document may be presented instead.                                                  |
| Owner's declaration        | A signed **declaration** confirming that the movement is **non-commercial**.                                                                                                                                                        |

The microchip should be implanted before or at the time the relevant veterinary procedures are carried out so that the animal can be reliably identified. The animal's identification details, rabies vaccination and, where applicable, the rabies antibody titration test should be recorded in the Health Certificate, EU pet passport or other recognised veterinary document.

The requirements for **rabies vaccination**, **rabies antibody titration testing** and any **parasite treatment** vary depending on the country or territory of departure and may change over time. Before travelling, always confirm the current veterinary requirements with the **National Food Agency** and do not rely solely on general guidance or unofficial sources.
## What the Health Certificate must contain

The Health Certificate (or, where accepted, the EU pet passport or other recognised veterinary document) must include, as applicable, at least the following information:

- the **location of the microchip (or tattoo), its identification code, and the date of implantation or reading**;
- the animal's **species, breed, date of birth, sex and colour**;
- the **certificate number**;
- the **owner's name and contact details**;
- the **name, contact details and signature of the certifying veterinarian**;
- details of the **rabies vaccination**;
- the **date of blood sampling** for the rabies antibody titration test, where applicable;
- confirmation of any **preventive veterinary measures** required under the applicable rules; and
- details of the **competent certifying and inspecting authority**, together with the inspection date.

The owner's signed **declaration** (Annex No. 5 to the Rules), confirming that the movement is **non-commercial**, must accompany the Health Certificate or other accepted veterinary document.

## Number of animals

As a general rule, no more than **five** cats, dogs or ferrets may accompany a single owner or authorised person as part of one non-commercial movement.

A larger number is permitted only where **all** of the following conditions are satisfied:

- the animals are travelling for the purpose of **competitions, exhibitions, sporting events**, or training connected with such events;
- the owner provides **written evidence** that the animals are registered for the relevant event or with the organising association; and
- the animals are **more than six months old**.

Where these conditions are not met, the movement falls outside the non-commercial regime and is subject to the veterinary import requirements and official controls applicable under Georgian legislation.
## Entry points and border controls

Pets should enter Georgia through a **customs checkpoint** authorised to carry out the relevant veterinary controls.

The **Revenue Service** is responsible for customs border procedures, while veterinary requirements are administered by the **National Food Agency (NFA)** in accordance with Georgian legislation.

On arrival, be prepared to present the **Health Certificate** (or, where accepted, the **EU pet passport** or other recognised veterinary document), together with the owner's signed **non-commercial declaration** and any other supporting documents requested by the competent authorities.

Before travelling, confirm the current entry procedures and documentary requirements with the **National Food Agency** or the **Revenue Service**, as administrative procedures may change.

## Before you travel

Before travelling to Georgia with your pet:

- confirm the current **rabies vaccination** and, where applicable, **rabies antibody titration test** requirements for your country or territory of departure;
- make sure the **microchip complies with the applicable ISO standards** and that its identification number matches all veterinary documents;
- obtain a properly completed **Health Certificate** (or, where accepted, an **EU pet passport** or other recognised veterinary document), together with the required **non-commercial declaration**; and
- check that the number of animals travelling complies with the **five-animal limit**, unless one of the statutory exceptions applies.
