This guide covers motor insurance in Georgia: the compulsory policy for cars on foreign plates, the voluntary options for cars registered here, the planned reform, and what to do after an accident.

For licences, foreign plates and the 90-day rule, see **[Driving in Georgia](/en/driving-in-georgia)**. For customs clearance and registration, see **[Importing & Registering a Car](/en/importing-a-car-to-georgia)**.

## The short version

| Your situation | Insurance required? | What to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Car on foreign plates, driving into Georgia | **Yes**, for the whole stay | Georgian MTPL, from GEL 30 for 15 days |
| Car registered in Georgia | **No** (as of November 2026) | Voluntary third-party liability and/or CASCO |
| Rental car | Included by the rental company | Check the excess and what is excluded |
| Car bought on credit | Usually required by the bank | CASCO, as set out in the loan agreement |

## Compulsory MTPL for foreign-registered vehicles

Since **1 March 2018**, the owner or driver of any vehicle **registered in another country** must insure their civil liability in Georgia for the **entire time the vehicle is in the country**. The rule comes from a dedicated law adopted on 15 December 2017 and applies to cars, motorcycles over 50 cc, buses, trucks, trailers and self-propelled machinery.

- The policy is tied to the **vehicle**, not to a named driver, so anyone driving the car is covered.
- Cover starts when the premium is paid and ends at **24:00 on the last day** of the policy.
- Policies are issued on paper or electronically.
- The law lists **no exemption for Green Card** or other foreign policies, so a Georgian policy is needed even if you are insured at home.

### Prices

Premiums are **fixed by law** (Article 5), so the price is the same wherever you buy.

| Vehicle | 15 days | 30 days | 90 days | 1 year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Passenger car (up to 3.5 t, up to 8 seats) | GEL 30 | GEL 50 | GEL 90 | GEL 295 |
| Motorcycle (over 50 cc) | GEL 20 | GEL 35 | GEL 70 | GEL 215 |
| Bus (over 8 seats) | GEL 45 | GEL 75 | GEL 140 | GEL 480 |
| Truck (over 3.5 t) | GEL 60 | GEL 100 | GEL 170 | GEL 610 |
| Trailer | GEL 14 | GEL 25 | GEL 40 | GEL 145 |
| Agricultural or special machinery | GEL 25 | GEL 45 | GEL 70 | GEL 250 |

A foreign-plated car can stay in Georgia for **90 days** before it must leave or go through customs, so the 90-day policy usually matches the stay.

### What it covers

The policy pays **other people** for harm you cause. It does not pay for damage to your own car.

| Type of harm | Limit per injured person | Limit per accident |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Injury, disability or death | GEL 30,000 (of which up to GEL 15,000 for treatment) | GEL 300,000 |
| Damage to property | GEL 25,000 | GEL 50,000 |

Not covered: harm during races or training on closed sites, harm the victim caused deliberately, force majeure, nuclear or hazardous cargo, and war or terrorism (Article 4).

### Where to buy

The scheme is run by the **Compulsory Insurance Center** ([tpl.ge](https://www.tpl.ge/en)). You can buy a policy:

- online on tpl.ge;
- at the Center's sales points and through insurance agents and brokers, including at border crossings;
- through banks and Paybox payment terminals.

Border officers (Patrol Police and Customs) are required to tell you about the obligation when you enter. Keep proof of the policy in the car.

### Fine and uninsured drivers

Driving a foreign-registered vehicle without the policy is an administrative offence with a **fine of GEL 100–200**.

If an **uninsured foreign car** injures you or damages your property, the Center pays you on the same terms as an insured claim and then claims the money back from the driver at fault (Articles 9–10).

## Cars registered in Georgia

As of **November 2026**, there is **no compulsory motor insurance** for vehicles registered in Georgia. Most cars on Georgian roads are uninsured, which matters when someone else causes an accident: without their insurance, you would have to recover damages from the driver directly, usually through the courts.

Private insurers sell two main products:

- **Voluntary third-party liability (TPL).** Covers harm you cause to others. Limits and prices vary by insurer and are typically set per policy, not by law.
- **CASCO.** Covers damage to or theft of **your own car**, often with an excess (deductible). Price depends on the car's value, age, the driver's experience and the excess. Banks usually require CASCO on cars bought with a loan.

Motor insurance is sold by the large Georgian insurers, including **Aldagi**, **GPI Holding**, **TBC Insurance**, **Ardi**, **Irao** and **Imedi L**, as well as through bank apps and brokers. Compare the limits, the excess, whether assistance and towing are included, and which repair shops the insurer uses.

## Planned compulsory MTPL for every car

The government has been preparing a law to make third-party liability insurance **compulsory for every vehicle owner in Georgia**, in line with the EU Motor Insurance Directive (2009/103/EC) under the Association Agreement.

- The draft was put on the government's legislative plan for the **spring 2026** session and again for the **autumn 2026** session.
- Under the plan, victims would be compensated even when the driver at fault is **uninsured or unidentified**.
- The Georgian Insurers Association has estimated the premium at around **GEL 20–25 a month** (about GEL 250 a year). This is an estimate, not an official tariff.
- As of **November 2026** the law **has not been adopted**, and no start date, limits or fines have been fixed.

We will update this page when Parliament votes on the bill.

## After an accident

1. **Stop and make the scene safe.** Do not move the cars if someone is injured, unless they are blocking traffic dangerously.
2. **Call 112.** Patrol police attend and record the accident; insurers generally rely on the police record to decide who was at fault.
3. **Stay at the scene** until the police arrive, unless someone needs urgent medical help.
4. **Call your insurer.** For a foreign-registered car, call the Compulsory Insurance Center hotline **+995 32 2 30 11 11**. For a voluntary policy, call the number on your policy.
5. **Collect evidence.** Take photos of the cars, plates, road and damage, and get the other driver's name, ID, phone number and insurance details if they have any.

## Rental cars

Rental companies in Georgia include insurance in the rental price, but cover varies. Check the **excess** you would pay after damage, whether tyres, glass and the underbody are covered, and whether you may take the car into the mountains or across the border. If you drive a Georgian rental car into a neighbouring country, you will need that country's insurance as well.

Insurance rules and prices can change. Confirm the current terms with the Compulsory Insurance Center or your insurer before relying on them.
