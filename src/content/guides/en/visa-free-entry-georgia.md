Georgia has one of the most open entry regimes in the world: if you hold a passport from one of the listed countries, you can arrive with no visa, no application and no fee, and stay for a full year. This page explains exactly who qualifies, what "one year" means in practice, and — just as important — what visa-free entry does **not** give you.

## The rule in one line

Under **Ordinance of the Government of Georgia No 255 of 5 June 2015**, citizens of the listed countries "may enter and stay in Georgia without visa for one full year." That is 365 days from entry — not the 90-days-in-180 limit common in Europe. The ordinance is issued under Articles 4(4) and 10(1) and (3) of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons.

## Who qualifies

The annex to Ordinance No 255 lists more than 90 countries. It includes:

- **All EU Member States** and the wider EEA (plus Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein).
- **The United Kingdom**, the **United States**, **Canada**, **Australia** and **New Zealand**.
- **The Gulf states** — the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman.
- Most of the **former Soviet space** — Ukraine, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Belarus, Moldova, Russia — as well as Turkey, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia and many countries in Latin America and the Caribbean.

The list is periodically amended; the most recent consolidated version is dated **24 February 2026**. Because countries can be added or removed, always confirm your own nationality against the current text before you travel.

Two extra rules sit alongside the main list. EU citizens may enter on a **national ID card** (showing name, surname, date of birth and photo), not only a passport. And holders of a **UN Laissez-Passer** get the same one-year visa-free entry.

## What "one full year" means — and the border-hop reset

The 365 days run **from each entry**. Georgian law does not attach a rolling cap (like the Schengen 90/180) or a mandatory cooling-off period to the visa-free regime, so in practice the clock restarts every time you enter. This is why so many long-stayers do a short "**border run**" — crossing to Armenia, Turkey or Azerbaijan for a few hours — and re-enter for a fresh year.

Two cautions matter here:

- **Entry is never guaranteed.** Admission is decided by the border officer under the Law on Aliens. They may refuse entry to someone who appears to be using back-to-back visa-free stays to live in Georgia indefinitely instead of regularising status. Refusals are uncommon but real.
- **If you overstay**, you are in the country unlawfully. That is an administrative offence and can bring a fine and complications on your next entry. If you plan to stay beyond a year, don't rely on an overstay — leave and re-enter, or move to a residence permit.

## A separate rule for visa/residence-permit holders

Even if your nationality is **not** on the one-year list, you may still enter visa-free for a **short stay** if you hold a valid visa or residence permit from certain countries (such as the EU, the US and others). This is set out in a companion government ordinance and typically allows up to **90 days in any 180-day period** — useful for nationals who would otherwise need a Georgian visa but already hold, say, a US visa or an EU residence card.

## What visa-free entry does NOT give you

This is where people get caught out. Being visa-free means you are **lawfully present** — nothing more:

- **It is not a residence permit.** To settle in Georgia on a stable, renewable basis (and to build toward permanent residence or citizenship), you need a residence permit, which flows from a long-term D visa rather than from visa-free entry.
- **It does not grant the right to work.** Since **1 March 2026**, paid employment, self-employment and entrepreneurial activity generally require a separate "Right to Labour Activity" permit. Entering visa-free does not exempt you.
- **It does not make you a tax resident — or stop you becoming one.** Tax residency turns on physical presence of **183 days or more** in any rolling 12-month period, independent of your immigration status. You can be visa-free and still cross the tax-residency threshold, so track your days if that matters to you.

## When to move beyond visa-free

Visa-free entry is ideal for visiting, testing the waters, or a stay of up to a year with no local work. If your plans include working for a Georgian client, registering as an Individual Entrepreneur, enrolling your children in school long-term, or simply putting your status on a firmer footing, the next step is a **residence permit** (see the Residence Permits guide) and, if you will earn income here, a **work permit** (see the Right to Work guide).
