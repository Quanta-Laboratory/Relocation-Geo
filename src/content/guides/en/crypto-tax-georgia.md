Georgia is one of the few countries where an individual can sell cryptocurrency at a profit and **owe no income tax** on the gain. The rule is well established and rests on a single, specific instrument: **Public Ruling N201 of the Minister of Finance, issued on 28 June 2019**. This guide explains what that ruling actually says, where its limits lie — including the common assumption that *all* crypto activity is tax-free — and how it fits alongside Georgia's separate licensing rules for crypto businesses. The substance is checked against the **primary source**.

## The headline: no income tax on individual crypto gains

Under **Public Ruling N201**, a **natural person is exempt from personal income tax on the profit from selling cryptocurrency**. There is no holding-period condition and no special crypto-gains rate to apply — for an individual, the gain on disposing of crypto-assets simply falls outside the personal income tax.

This is the core of Georgia's appeal to individual crypto holders, and it sits on firm ground: a public ruling is a **binding clarification** of the Tax Code (see below), not an informal statement of practice.

## VAT: crypto-to-fiat is exempt

The ruling also settles the **VAT** question. **Exchanging cryptocurrency into national currencies** — lari, dollars, euros and so on — is **not subject to VAT**, and this applies to **both individuals and companies**. Converting your crypto to cash does not create a VAT charge.

## Mining and computing power

Public Ruling N201 deals separately with **computing (hash) power**, which matters for miners:

Selling computing power **from Georgia abroad** is **not subject to VAT**, and the **right to credit input VAT** on those operations is **retained** — a favourable combination.

Selling computing power **within Georgia**, from one Georgian resident to another, **is a VAT-taxable** operation.

The income-tax treatment of mining proceeds is more fact-dependent — it turns on the scale of the activity and on whether you operate as an individual or through a company — so miners should take specific advice rather than assume the individual gains exemption automatically covers operational mining income.

## What "public ruling" means — and why you can rely on it

It is worth being precise about the legal status, because the crypto exemption lives in a ruling rather than in the Tax Code itself. A **public ruling** is an instrument **provided for by the Tax Code** through which the **Minister of Finance clarifies** how tax legislation applies. Its protective effect is concrete: if a person **acts in accordance with** a public ruling, the monitoring and enforcement authorities **may not adopt a decision contradicting it** and **may not impose additional tax or sanctions** for that conduct. N201 is the main normative act addressing crypto-asset taxation in Georgia.

## The limits: what is *not* exempt

The exemption is narrower than the slogan "crypto is tax-free in Georgia" suggests, and two limits catch people out.

First, the relief is on **disposing of crypto-assets** — a capital-gains-style exemption. It does **not** make **income you earn** tax-free just because it is paid in crypto. If you **perform services while in Georgia and are paid in cryptocurrency**, that is ordinary **Georgian-source income**, taxable under the normal rules — the flat **20% [personal income tax](https://relocation.ge/en/personal-income-tax-georgia/)**, or a **small-business rate** if you qualify. Crypto is merely the form of payment; it does not change what the income is.

Second, the individual income-tax exemption is for **natural persons**. **Companies** are taxed under Georgia's **corporate distributed-profit system**, so trading crypto through a Georgian company is a different analysis (even though the **VAT** treatment of crypto-to-fiat exchange applies to companies too). Model the corporate position separately before structuring crypto activity through an entity.

## Holding crypto vs. running a crypto business: the VASP line

Finally, keep two regimes apart. **Public Ruling N201 is about tax.** Georgia's **Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP)** regime is about **licensing and supervision** of businesses that provide crypto services to others — exchanges, custodians and the like — and is run by the **National Bank of Georgia**.

An individual who simply **holds and sells their own crypto** is **not** a VASP and is not caught by that regime. A business that **offers crypto services to others** may need to **register**. If that is your situation, read the dedicated **[VASP guide](https://relocation.ge/en/vasp-georgia/)** alongside this one.

This guide is general information, **not personal tax advice**. Crypto tax outcomes depend on whether you act as an individual or a company, on how income is sourced, and on facts that ruling N201 does not exhaustively cover — so take professional advice before relying on a particular treatment.
