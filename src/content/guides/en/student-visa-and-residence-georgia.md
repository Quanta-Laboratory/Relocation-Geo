Studying in Georgia is recognised as a long-term immigration purpose. Foreign nationals who come to Georgia for study generally rely on the **immigration (D)** visa framework and, where eligible, may later apply for a **study residence permit**.

## The D3 immigration visa

The **D3 visa** is Georgia's immigration visa for **adult** students and researchers. It is issued to foreign nationals who come to Georgia to study or conduct research at an **authorised educational institution**, as well as those participating in recognised international educational programmes.

Like all **D-category visas**, the D3 is a **multiple-entry immigration visa**. It is intended for foreign nationals relocating to Georgia for long-term educational purposes and may serve as the basis for applying for a **study residence permit**, provided the statutory requirements are met.

For an overview of all visa categories, see the **[Guide to Georgian Visas](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-georgian-visas/)**.

## Do you need a D3 visa before arriving?

**Not always.**

Many foreign students do **not** need to obtain a D3 visa before travelling. Citizens of countries eligible for **visa-free entry**may lawfully enter Georgia, enrol at an authorised educational institution and, if they meet the legal requirements, apply for a **study residence permit** from within Georgia.

Foreign nationals whose nationality **requires a visa** will generally need to obtain a **D3 visa** before travelling to Georgia by applying through the appropriate **Georgian embassy or consulate** in accordance with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' visa procedures.

In all cases, the educational institution must be **authorised** under Georgian law. Study at an unauthorised institution will not qualify a foreign national for a study residence permit.

## Applying for a study residence permit

If you intend to remain in Georgia for the duration of your studies, you should apply for a **study (temporary) residence permit**.

A study residence permit is issued for the purpose of studying at an **authorised educational institution** in Georgia. It is granted for a limited period and may be renewed while the legal grounds for its issuance continue to exist. For most students, the residence permit—not the entry visa—is the document that authorises a multi-year stay in Georgia.

For a general overview of the different residence-permit categories, see the **[Guide to Residence Permits](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-residence-permits/)**.

## Where to apply

Applications for a study residence permit are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia (PSDA)**. They may be filed in person through a **Public Service Hall**, electronically through the Agency's online services, or in another manner permitted by law. The Agency is responsible for examining the application and deciding whether to grant or extend the residence permit.

The key supporting document is a **certificate issued by an authorised educational institution** confirming that you are enrolled and stating the anticipated duration of your studies. Applicants must also provide proof of lawful stay in Georgia, identity documents, evidence of sufficient financial means and the other documents required by law. Because a study residence permit is linked to your student status, you should ensure that your enrolment remains valid throughout your studies.

## Who qualifies

A **study residence permit** may be granted to a foreign national who is studying at an **authorised educational institution in Georgia**.

This includes students enrolled at:

- **higher education institutions** (universities);
- **vocational education institutions**; and
- **general education institutions**, where permitted by the Law.

The applicant must remain enrolled at the authorised educational institution throughout the period for which the residence permit is sought.

## Duration

A study residence permit is issued for the **anticipated period of study**, subject to the limits established by Georgian law.

As with other temporary residence permits, the **first permit** is generally issued for a period of **6 months to 1 year**. It may be **extended** while the legal grounds for its issuance continue to exist, provided that the **total period** of the temporary residence permit does not exceed **six years**.

## Study residence permit: documents and fees

The standard supporting documents for a **study residence permit** include:

- a completed **application**;
- a **copy of the passport** or other valid travel document;
- a **document confirming lawful stay** in Georgia;
- a **certificate issued by an authorised educational institution** in Georgia confirming the applicant's enrolment and stating the **anticipated duration of the study programme**;
- **evidence of sufficient financial means**. This may consist of a document confirming the legal income of the applicant, a Georgian citizen, or the applicant's relative residing in Georgia under a residence permit (together with proof of the family relationship, where applicable). Alternatively, the applicant may rely on **funds held in their own bank account**, confirmed by a bank statement or bank certificate, provided the available funds correspond to **at least twice the monthly subsistence minimum of an average consumer in Georgia for each month of the requested residence-permit period**;
- a **colour photograph (3 × 4 cm)** in electronic format;
- **proof of payment** of the applicable residence-permit service fee.

Foreign public documents generally need to be **legalised or apostilled** (unless an international treaty or Georgian legislation provides otherwise), translated into **Georgian**, and the translation **notarised**.

The applicable **state fee** depends on the selected processing time:

| Processing time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | GEL 300 |
| 20 calendar days | GEL 450 |
| 10 calendar days | GEL 600 |

## Georgian ID (residence) card

Once a **Georgian residence permit** has been granted, the foreign national must obtain a **Georgian ID (residence) card**.

The residence card serves as the official identity document for foreign nationals residing in Georgia under a residence permit. It is issued by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** upon application submitted through one of the Agency's territorial offices or a **Public Service Hall**.

The applicable **state fee** depends on the selected processing time:

| Issuance time | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 10 working days | GEL 60 |
| 5 working days | GEL 80 |
| 3 working days | GEL 100 |
| 2 working days | GEL 120 |
| Same day | GEL 150 |

## Insurance and other entry requirements

Foreign students should ensure that they meet all applicable **entry requirements** before travelling to Georgia.

Since **1 January 2026**, foreign nationals entering Georgia as visitors are generally required to hold **health and accident insurance** with a minimum coverage of **GEL 30,000** for the entire period of their stay. For full details, see the **[Health Insurance Guide](https://relocation.ge/en/health-insurance-in-georgia-for-foreigners/)**.

Students should also be prepared to demonstrate that they satisfy any applicable **entry conditions**, including having sufficient financial means and, where requested, evidence of their accommodation and educational arrangements.

## A note on changing rules

Immigration requirements and administrative procedures may change over time. Before travelling, you should confirm the latest visa, residence-permit and enrolment requirements with your **educational institution**, the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** and the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Georgia**.
