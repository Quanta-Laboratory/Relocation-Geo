Studying in Georgia is treated as a long-term purpose under the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons, so it runs on the immigration (D) side of the system rather than a tourist entry. In practice there are two steps: getting the right entry status, then converting it into a residence permit that lasts for your studies.

## The D3 immigration visa

The D3 category is the student visa. It is issued to people arriving to study or conduct research at an authorised educational institution in Georgia, and to those studying under international programmes. Like all D-category visas it is a long-term, multiple-entry visa, and — importantly — a long-term visa is the legal precondition for obtaining a residence permit. The detailed category list is in the [guide to Georgian visas](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-georgian-visas/).

## Do you need the visa before arriving?

Many students don't need to apply abroad first. Citizens of the 90-plus visa-free countries can enter Georgia and stay up to a year, which gives time to enrol and arrange a residence permit from within the country. Students whose nationality requires a visa apply for the D3 visa in advance, frequently through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs e-visa portal. Either way, the institution must be authorised — enrolment at an unauthorised institution will not support a study residence permit.

## From visa to study residence permit

To remain for the full length of a degree, you convert your status into a **study (temporary) residence permit**. It is tied to your enrolment, issued for a limited period, and renewed as your studies continue. The residence permit — not the entry visa — is what makes a multi-year stay lawful. Residence permits in general are covered in the [guide to residence permits](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-residence-permits/).

## Where it is processed

Study residence-permit applications and extensions are handled through the Public Service Hall and the relevant state agency. Applications are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia**. The core of any application is proof that you are enrolled at an authorised institution, alongside identity and financial documents. Keep your enrolment confirmation and academic status current, because the permit depends on them.

## Who qualifies

A study residence permit may be granted to a foreigner **enrolled in an educational institution authorised under Georgian legislation**. Eligible study covers:

- **higher education institutions** (universities);
- **vocational education institutions**;
- **other accredited educational programmes** recognised under Georgian law.

## Duration

A study residence permit is usually issued for **6 months to 1 year**. It **may be renewed** for the duration of the studies, provided the student continues their educational programme.

## Study residence permit: documents and fees

The typical document set for a study residence permit is:

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the passport** or travel document;
- a **document confirming lawful stay** in Georgia;
- a **certificate from an authorised educational institution** in Georgia confirming enrolment and indicating the **expected duration of the study programme**;
- a **document confirming legal income in Georgia** — of the foreigner, and/or of a Georgian citizen, or of the foreigner's relative resident in Georgia who holds a residence permit (with a document confirming the family relationship, where applicable). As proof of financial means, **funds held in the foreigner's own bank account** may also be accepted, confirmed by a bank statement or certificate, provided the amount corresponds to **at least twice the subsistence minimum of an average consumer in Georgia per month** for the duration of the requested permit;
- a **colour photograph, 3×4**, in electronic format;
- the **receipt** confirming payment of the residence-permit service fee.

Foreign documents must generally be **legalised or apostilled** and translated into Georgian and notarised, unless an exemption applies.

The state fee depends on how quickly the application is processed:

| Processing time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | GEL 300 |
| 20 calendar days | GEL 450 |
| 10 calendar days | GEL 600 |

## Georgian ID (residence) card

After obtaining a residence permit, a foreign national may apply for a **Georgian ID (residence) card** through the Public Service Hall or the Public Registry. The fee depends on the processing time:

| Issuance time | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 10 working days | GEL 60 |
| 5 working days | GEL 80 |
| 3 working days | GEL 100 |
| 2 working days | GEL 120 |
| Same day | GEL 150 |

## Insurance and other entry conditions

Since 1 January 2026, everyone entering Georgia must hold health and accident insurance with a minimum sum insured of GEL 30,000 for the duration of their stay — the details are in the [health insurance guide](https://relocation.ge/en/health-insurance-in-georgia-for-foreigners/). Students should also be ready to show proof of financial means and accommodation.

## A note on changing rules

Admission and immigration rules for international students are under review and can change between intakes. Confirm the current visa and residence-permit requirements with your university, the Public Service Hall and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs before you travel.

## Legal sources

The study residence-permit rules on this page are based on the **Law of Georgia on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government of Georgia Resolution No 520 of 1 September 2014** on the procedure for reviewing and deciding on the issuance of residence permits in Georgia. Study residence permits are issued by the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia**. Thresholds, fees and processing times are periodically updated, so confirm the current requirements with the agency before applying. Official versions of the legislation are published by the Legislative Herald of Georgia (Matsne).
