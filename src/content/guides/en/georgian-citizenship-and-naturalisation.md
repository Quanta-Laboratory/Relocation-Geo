Georgian citizenship is the legal bond between an individual and the Georgian state. The rules governing its acquisition are set out in the **Organic Law of Georgia on Georgian Citizenship**.

For most foreign nationals, the relevant route is **naturalisation**, although Georgian law also provides for citizenship by birth and several other statutory grounds. This guide focuses on the naturalisation process and the services provided by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)**.

If you are planning to settle in Georgia before applying for citizenship, you may also find the **[Guide to Residence Permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)** and the **[Permanent Residence Permit](/en/permanent-residence-permit-georgia)** guide helpful.

## How citizenship is acquired

The **Organic Law of Georgia on Georgian Citizenship** provides that Georgian citizenship may be acquired:

- **by birth**;
- **by naturalisation**; or
- **by restoration**, in the cases provided by law.

### Citizenship by birth

Citizenship is acquired by birth in the circumstances provided by the Organic Law, including, for example:

- a child at least one of whose parents is a **Georgian citizen** at the time of birth; and
- certain children born in Georgia to **stateless persons** holding the relevant legal status in Georgia.

### Citizenship by naturalisation

Naturalisation may take one of the following forms:

| Type of naturalisation    | Who it is for (in outline)                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Regular procedure**     | Adults with long lawful residence in Georgia who meet the knowledge and other conditions                                                                                  |
| **Simplified procedure**  | A spouse of a Georgian citizen (after qualifying residence); persons with repatriate status                                                                               |
| **Exceptional procedure** | Persons granted Georgian citizenship by the **President of Georgia** because of exceptional merit before Georgia or where granting citizenship is in the state's interest |

### Restoration of citizenship

**Restoration** is a separate legal ground for acquiring Georgian citizenship. It is available to certain **former Georgian citizens** whose citizenship has ceased, provided they satisfy the conditions.

## Regular naturalisation — the main route

For most foreign nationals, **regular naturalisation** is the principal route to acquiring Georgian citizenship.

An adult may be granted Georgian citizenship under the regular procedure if they satisfy **all** of the following conditions:

- they have **lawfully resided in Georgia for the 10 consecutive years immediately preceding the application** (or **5 consecutive years** for a **stateless person holding status in Georgia**);
- they demonstrate **knowledge of the Georgian state language** to the standard established by law;
- they demonstrate **knowledge of the history of Georgia and the basic principles of Georgian law** to the standard established by law; and
- they have a **job in Georgia**, **own real estate in Georgia**, **carry on entrepreneurial activity in Georgia**, or **hold an interest or shares in a Georgian enterprise**.

### What "continuous residence" means

For the purposes of Georgian citizenship, **continuous residence** means residence in Georgia **without spending an aggregate period of more than 90 days outside Georgia during any one-year period**.

An absence from Georgia of **up to two years** for the purposes of **education or medical treatment** does **not** interrupt continuous residence and is **not counted** towards the 90-day limit.

For these purposes, a **year** means a period of **365 consecutive days** (or **366 consecutive days** in a leap year).

### The citizenship test

Before applying for **Georgian citizenship by naturalisation**, applicants must demonstrate **knowledge of the Georgian state language, the history of Georgia and the basic principles of Georgian law**.

The knowledge test is taken **before** the citizenship application is submitted, and the **test result remains valid for one year**.

According to the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)**, the examination is **free of charge on the first attempt**. If an applicant does not pass, the fee for the **second and each subsequent attempt** is **GEL 40**.

A person with a **persistent physical, mental, intellectual and/or sensory impairment** that makes it impossible to take the examination may be **exempted from the knowledge test by the Citizenship Commission**, in accordance with the Organic Law.

### Oath of allegiance

Applicants who are granted **Georgian citizenship by naturalisation** are generally required to take an **oath of allegiance to Georgia** in the **Georgian language**, unless they fall within one of the exceptions provided by law.

## Simplified naturalisation

A person **married to a Georgian citizen**may acquire Georgian citizenship under the **simplified naturalisation procedure** if they:

- have **continuously and lawfully resided in Georgia for the five consecutive years immediately preceding the application**; and
- demonstrate **knowledge of the Georgian state language, the history of Georgia and the basic principles of Georgian law** to the standard established by law.

Persons holding **repatriate status** may also acquire Georgian citizenship under a **simplified naturalisation procedure**, in accordance with the rules established by the **Citizenship Commission**.

## Naturalisation by way of exception

The **President of Georgia** may grant Georgian citizenship **by way of exception** to:

- a foreign national who has rendered **services of exceptional merit to Georgia**; or
- a foreign national where the **grant of Georgian citizenship is in the state's interest**.

When determining whether granting citizenship is in the **state's interest**, the Organic Law provides that relevant circumstances may include, for example:

- the applicant considers **Georgia to be their homeland** (such as certain displaced persons or members of the historic Georgian diaspora);
- the applicant has made an **investment that makes a significant contribution to the Georgian economy**; or
- the applicant has achieved **distinction in sport, science or the arts** and wishes to pursue their activities on behalf of Georgia.

Unlike the ordinary and simplified naturalisation procedures, **citizenship granted by way of exception may allow the applicant to retain their existing foreign citizenship**, where the conditions of the Organic Law are satisfied.

## Restoration of citizenship

Georgian citizenship may be acquired **by way of restoration** by a person whose Georgian citizenship was terminated:

- **unlawfully**;
- **following renunciation of Georgian citizenship**; or
- **as a result of the choice made by a parent or other legal representative**.

As a general rule, restoration requires the applicant to demonstrate **knowledge of the Georgian state language** and to satisfy the conditions relating to the **absence of the refusal grounds**. 

These requirements do **not** apply where the applicant's Georgian citizenship was **terminated unlawfully**.

The Organic Law also provides a **special transitional restoration procedure** for certain former Georgian citizens who lost their citizenship after acquiring the citizenship of another country. As the availability of this procedure is subject to statutory deadlines that have been amended over time, applicants should confirm the **current eligibility criteria and applicable deadline** with the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** before applying.

## Dual citizenship: Georgia's approach

As a general rule, Georgia follows the **principle of single citizenship**. A citizen of Georgia may simultaneously hold the citizenship of another country **only in the cases provided for by the Organic Law**.

In practice, this means:

- **Acquiring the citizenship of another country** generally results in the **loss of Georgian citizenship**, unless the Organic Law provides otherwise.
- A Georgian citizen may **retain Georgian citizenship** when acquiring a foreign citizenship if they obtain the **State's prior consent**. The decision is based on the statutory criteria, including whether the applicant's connection with Georgia is considered reliable.
- A **foreign national acquiring Georgian citizenship by regular or simplified naturalisation** will generally be required to **renounce their existing citizenship**. The presidential act granting Georgian citizenship takes effect only after the competent authority receives confirmation that the applicant has renounced their previous citizenship.
- By contrast, a person granted **Georgian citizenship by way of exception** may, in the circumstances provided by the Organic Law, **retain their existing foreign citizenship**.

As the rules governing multiple citizenship have been amended over time, applicants should confirm the **current legal requirements and administrative practice** with the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** before making decisions based on their citizenship status.

## Grounds for refusal

An applicant **may not be granted Georgian citizenship by naturalisation** if they:

- have committed an **international crime against peace and humanity**;
- have committed a **grave crime** against an individual, the state, or **public security and public order**, as defined by Georgian legislation;
- are considered to pose a risk such that **granting Georgian citizenship is contrary to the interests of state security and/or public security**; or
- are **wanted** by the law-enforcement authorities of Georgia or another country, or by **INTERPOL**, and/or are the subject of **extradition or expulsion proceedings**.

## Who decides, and how long it takes

Applications for **Georgian citizenship** are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** or, if the applicant is abroad, to a **Georgian diplomatic mission or consular office**.

The application is examined by the **Public Service Development Agency** with the assistance of the **Citizenship Commission**, an advisory body composed of representatives of the **PSDA**, the **State Security Service of Georgia** and the **Administration of the President of Georgia**.

Following its examination, the Agency submits its opinion to the **President of Georgia**, who makes the **final decision** on granting, restoring, retaining, refusing or terminating Georgian citizenship, as provided by the Organic Law.

A decision on the application is made **within a period not exceeding three months**.

If an application for **naturalisation** is refused, the applicant may submit a new application on the **same grounds** **six months** after the refusal.

## State fees

Applicants pay a **service fee** in the amount and under the procedure **prescribed by the Government of Georgia**. The applicable fees and processing options are published by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)**.

As currently listed by the PSDA for **regular naturalisation**:

| Decision time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 80th day | GEL 65 |
| 60th day | GEL 130 |
| 40th day | GEL 260 |
| 20th day | GEL 390 |

The PSDA notes that the **80-day regular naturalisation service is free of charge for stateless persons holding stateless-person status in Georgia**.

Fees differ for other procedures. For example, the PSDA currently lists:

- **Naturalisation by way of exception:** **GEL 500 / 700 / 1,000** (80th / 60th / 30th day); and
- **Restoration of citizenship:** **GEL 130 / 195** (30th / 20th day).

As the Government may amend the applicable fees, applicants should confirm the **current service fees and processing times** with the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** before submitting an application.

## How this relates to residence


A **Georgian residence permit** allows a foreign national to live in Georgia lawfully, but it does **not** confer Georgian citizenship.

For applicants seeking citizenship through **regular naturalisation**, the key requirement is a period of **continuous lawful residence** in Georgia. The years spent residing lawfully in the country count towards this statutory residence requirement. Many long-term residents obtain a **permanent residence permit**, although this is **not** a legal prerequisite for acquiring citizenship.

Georgian citizenship provides rights and status that a residence permit cannot, including the right to obtain a **Georgian passport**, participate fully in Georgia's political life and enjoy the rights and obligations of a **Georgian citizen**. As a general rule, applicants acquiring Georgian citizenship through regular or simplified naturalisation will also need to address the rules on **multiple citizenship**, including, where applicable, the renunciation of their existing citizenship.

For more information on the immigration status that may precede citizenship, see the **[Permanent Residence Permit](/en/permanent-residence-permit-georgia)** and the **[Guide to Residence Permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.
