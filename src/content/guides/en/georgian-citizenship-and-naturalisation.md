Georgian citizenship is the legal bond between a person and the Georgian state. It is acquired in one of two ways — **by birth** or **by naturalisation** — and, for foreign nationals, the practical route is naturalisation. This guide summarises the framework set out in the **Organic Law of Georgia on Georgian Citizenship** and the services operated by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)**. It follows on from lawful residence: see the **[permanent residence permit](/en/permanent-residence-permit-georgia)** and the **[guide to residence permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)** for the status that comes first.

## How citizenship is acquired

The Organic Law recognises two forms of acquiring Georgian citizenship:

- **By birth** — including a child at least one of whose parents is a Georgian citizen at the time of birth, and certain children born in Georgia to stateless persons holding a status here.
- **By naturalisation** — citizenship granted by the **President of Georgia**.

Naturalisation itself has four types:

| Type of naturalisation | Who it is for (in outline) |
| --- | --- |
| **Regular procedure** | Adults with long lawful residence in Georgia who meet the knowledge and other conditions |
| **Simplified procedure** | A spouse of a Georgian citizen (after qualifying residence); persons with repatriate status |
| **By way of exception** | Persons granted citizenship by the President for exceptional merit or state interest |
| **By way of restoration** | Former Georgian citizens whose citizenship was terminated |

## Regular naturalisation — the main route

Under **Article 12** of the Organic Law, an adult is granted citizenship of Georgia under the regular procedure if they meet **all** of the following conditions:

- they have **lawfully resided in Georgia for the last 10 consecutive years** up to the day of applying (for a **stateless person holding a status in Georgia**, the period is the **last 5 years**);
- they **know the state language of Georgia** within the established limits;
- they **know the history of Georgia and the basic principles of law** within the established limits; and
- they have a **job and/or real estate in Georgia, carry on business in Georgia, or hold an interest or shares in a Georgian enterprise**.

### What "continuous residence" means

The law defines continuous residence in Georgia as residence **without spending a combined total of more than 90 days per year outside Georgia**. Time spent abroad of **up to two years for education or medical treatment** is not counted against this. A "year" for this purpose means 365 consecutive days (366 in a leap year).

### The citizenship test

Knowledge of the **state language, the history of Georgia and the basic principles of law** is checked **before** the citizenship application is submitted, and the **test result is valid for 1 year**. According to the PSDA, the exam is **free of charge the first time**, and **GEL 40 for the second and each subsequent attempt**. A person with a consistent physical, psychological, intellectual and/or sensory impairment that makes testing impossible may be **exempted by the Citizenship Commission**.

New citizens (other than the limited exceptions in the law) take an **oath of allegiance** to Georgia in the Georgian language.

## Simplified naturalisation

Under **Article 14**, a person **married to a Georgian citizen** who has **continuously lawfully resided in Georgia for the last 5 years** up to the day of applying may acquire citizenship under the **simplified procedure**, provided they pass the language, history and basics-of-law test. Persons holding **repatriate status** are also naturalised under a simplified procedure defined by the Citizenship Commission.

## Naturalisation by way of exception

Under **Article 17**, the **President of Georgia** may grant citizenship **by way of exception** to a foreign citizen who has made a **contribution of exceptional merit to Georgia**, or to an alien **based on state interests**. When assessing state interest, the law lists factors including that the person considers Georgia their homeland (for example a displaced person or a historic émigré), that they have made an **investment contributing substantially to the state economy**, or that they are **successful in sport, science or art** and willing to work on behalf of Georgia. This is the one naturalisation route where a person may **hold Georgian and foreign citizenship at the same time**.

## Restoration of citizenship

Under **Article 18**, citizenship is granted **by way of restoration** to persons whose Georgian citizenship was terminated **unlawfully**, **by renunciation**, or **as a result of a parent's choice**. Restoration generally requires knowledge of the **state language** and the absence of any Article 16 refusal ground; where citizenship was terminated **unlawfully**, those requirements do not apply. The law also provides a **time-limited transitional right** for former citizens who lost Georgian citizenship by acquiring a foreign one to apply for restoration; because this window has been extended repeatedly, **confirm the current deadline and conditions with the PSDA**.

## Dual citizenship: Georgia's position

Georgia is, in principle, a **single-citizenship** country. Under **Article 3** of the Organic Law, there is a single citizenship in Georgia, and a citizen of Georgia may simultaneously hold a foreign citizenship **only** in the cases provided for in the law. In practice this means:

- **Acquiring a foreign citizenship normally causes loss of Georgian citizenship** (Article 21), unless the person was naturalised by way of exception or obtained the state's consent to retain Georgian citizenship.
- A Georgian citizen may **retain Georgian citizenship** when naturalising abroad only if they obtain the **state's consent before** acquiring the foreign citizenship, and only where their connection with Georgia is deemed reliable (Article 21¹).
- A **foreign national naturalising in Georgia** (other than by way of exception) generally must **renounce their previous citizenship** — the President's decree granting citizenship takes effect once the competent authorities receive confirmation of that renunciation (Article 15).

Because dual-citizenship policy is politically sensitive and has changed several times, **confirm the current rules with the PSDA** before relying on them.

## Grounds for refusal

Under **Article 16**, a person is **not** granted citizenship by naturalisation if they:

- have committed an **international crime against peace and humanity**;
- have been involved in a **grave crime** against a person, the state, or public security and order under Georgian law;
- whose naturalisation is **inadvisable on state and/or public-security grounds**; or
- are **wanted** by the law-enforcement bodies of Georgia or another country, or by **INTERPOL**, and/or are subject to **extradition or expulsion** proceedings.

## Who decides, and how long it takes

Applications are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency** (or to a Georgian diplomatic mission or consular office abroad). The Agency considers them with the assistance of the **Citizenship Commission**, whose members are drawn from the Agency, the State Security Service and the Administration of the President. The Agency's opinion is forwarded to the **President of Georgia**, who makes the **final decision** to grant, retain, refuse or terminate citizenship.

The **time frame for a decision does not exceed three months**. If naturalisation is refused, the applicant may re-apply on the same grounds **6 months** after the negative decision.

## Service fees

State fees for citizenship services are set by the Government and published by the PSDA. As listed by the PSDA for the **regular procedure**:

| Decision time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 80th day | GEL 65 |
| 60th day | GEL 130 |
| 40th day | GEL 260 |
| 20th day | GEL 390 |

The PSDA notes that the 80th-day regular-procedure service is **free of charge for stateless persons holding a status in Georgia**. Fees differ for the other procedures — for example, the PSDA lists **GEL 500 / 700 / 1,000** for naturalisation **by way of exception** (80th / 60th / 30th day) and **GEL 130 / 195** for **restoration** (30th / 20th day). Fees change; **confirm the current figures with the PSDA** before applying.

## How this follows on from residence

Residence permits and permanent residence let a foreign national live in Georgia lawfully, but they are **not** citizenship. The years spent **lawfully resident** are what build the **continuous-residence record** on which regular naturalisation depends, and permanent residence is a common status to hold while that clock runs. Citizenship then adds a **Georgian passport and full political rights** — and, given the single-citizenship rule, usually means renouncing or reconciling an existing nationality. For the status that precedes citizenship, see the **[permanent residence permit](/en/permanent-residence-permit-georgia)** and **[guide to residence permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.
