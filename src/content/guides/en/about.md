Relocation.ge exists to answer one kind of question reliably: *what does Georgian law actually say about moving here, and where can I read it myself?*

Most information about relocating to Georgia comes from relocation agencies selling a service, forum threads that quietly go out of date, or AI-generated articles with no sources at all. People make real decisions on top of it — whether to apply for a residence permit, whether they will meet a deadline, which tax status fits their situation — and the cost of being wrong is theirs to carry. We built Relocation.ge to be the opposite of that: a reference you can trust because you can verify it.

## What makes it different

**Every page is tied to a primary source.** Each guide is built from the published law — the Legislative Herald (Matsne), the Public Service Development Agency, the Revenue Service, the Tax Code — and links straight to it. You are never more than one click from the text the guidance is based on.

**Two-date transparency.** Every page shows when it was last reviewed by a human and when it was last checked. You always know how fresh the information is, instead of guessing.

**Independent and non-commercial.** There are no affiliate deals, no "let us connect you to our lawyer for a fee," no advertiser deciding what we write. That independence lets us state things plainly — including the inconvenient parts, such as the fact that work performed in Georgia is Georgian-source income even when your clients are abroad.

**Structure over chaos.** Georgian law is scattered across many acts and is often published only in Georgian. We translate it into a navigable map: the residence-permit types, the tax regimes, how they connect and where they exclude one another. The work is curation, not copywriting.

## What this is — and what it is not

Relocation.ge is **structured information about the law as published. It is not legal advice.** Thresholds, fees, deadlines and procedures change, and individual situations differ. Use our pages to understand the framework and to find the primary source, then confirm the current position with the relevant authority or a qualified adviser before you rely on it.

## Where we are going

The near-term goal is to be the reference people genuinely trust: when someone searches a question about Georgia, they reach a page that gives them the answer *and* the source behind it. The longer-term aim is to become the default starting point for legal relocation to Georgia — a map of the law complete and current enough that newcomers, advisers and even AI assistants can rely on it as a primary layer. That is why the site also publishes a machine-readable [llms.txt](https://relocation.ge/llms.txt).

All of this rests on one discipline: keeping the content current as the law changes. For exactly how we source, review and version each page, see our [Methodology](https://relocation.ge/en/methodology/).

Relocation.ge is an independent, privately-run open-source project. It is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any government body.
