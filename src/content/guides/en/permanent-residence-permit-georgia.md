The **Permanent Residence Permit** is the goal for many long-term residents: the right to live in Georgia **indefinitely**, without the recurring renewals that temporary permits demand. It may be granted in specific cases defined by Georgian legislation, reached by two main routes — **family relations with a Georgian citizen** or **ten years of lawful residence**. This guide explains both routes, the required documents, the fees and how the permanent permit sits beside the investment-based indefinite permit — checked against the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government Resolution No. 520**.

## Who is eligible

A foreigner may obtain a Permanent Residence Permit in one of two cases.

**A. Family members of a Georgian citizen.** The permit may be granted to:

- the **spouse of a Georgian citizen**;
- the **minor child of a Georgian citizen**; and
- the **parent of a minor Georgian citizen**.

**B. Long-term residents of Georgia.** The permit may also be granted to a foreigner who has **legally lived in Georgia for the last ten years on the basis of a temporary residence permit**. Because the count is document-driven, keep your permits, residence cards, entry/exit records and address history so you can evidence the full period.

## Required documents

Applications are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia**. The document set depends on the qualifying route.

**Family members of a Georgian citizen:**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport** or travel document;
- a document confirming **lawful stay in Georgia**;
- a document confirming the **family relationship** with the Georgian citizen;
- a document confirming **legal income in Georgia** — of the foreigner, and/or the Georgian citizen, or a relative residing in Georgia who holds a residence permit. If the Georgian citizen is of **pension age or receives social assistance**, a document confirming **state pension or social assistance** may be submitted. Funds held in the **foreigner's personal bank account** may also be considered as income;
- a **colour photograph, 3×4**, in electronic format;
- the **receipt** confirming payment of the service fee.

**Foreigners who have lived in Georgia for ten years:**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport** or travel document;
- a document confirming **residence in Georgia for the last ten years** on the basis of a temporary residence permit (copy of the residence permit / residence card);
- a document confirming **legal income and family relationship** in Georgia, where applicable. If the Georgian citizen sponsor is of **pension age or receives social assistance**, a document confirming **state pension or social assistance** may be submitted. Funds held in the **foreigner's personal bank account** may also be considered as income;
- a **colour photograph, 3×4**, in electronic format;
- the **receipt** confirming payment of the service fee.

## What the permit does — and does not — give you

Permanent residence is granted for an **indefinite period** and removes the renewal cycle, putting the holder on a long-term footing close to that of a citizen for the purpose of living in the country. But it is **not citizenship**: there is no Georgian passport or voting right, and the status can still be **lost** in defined circumstances, such as prolonged absence or other grounds set out in the law. You continue to hold and replace the **physical residence (ID) card** on its own validity.

## Permanent vs. indefinite

Georgia has two indefinite-stay permits and they are reached differently. The **Permanent Residence Permit** here is for **family members of citizens** or **ten-year residents**. The **[Indefinite Residence Permit](/en/indefinite-residence-permit-georgia)** is the onward step for holders of an **[investment residence permit](/en/investment-residence-permit-georgia)** who have met the turnover or property conditions over five years. Both end the renewal treadmill; the qualifying path differs. For an overview of all the permit types, see the **[guide to residence permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.

## Processing time and state fees

The service fee for issuing a Permanent Residence Permit depends on how quickly the application is processed:

| Processing time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | GEL 350 |
| 20 calendar days | GEL 500 |

Establish your qualifying basis — proof of the family relationship and legal income, or evidence of the ten qualifying years — and file **before any current permit expires**. Confirm current fees and document lists with the **Public Service Development Agency**.

## Georgian ID (residence) card

After obtaining the residence permit, a foreign national may apply for a **Georgian ID (residence) card** through the Public Service Hall or the Public Registry. The fee depends on the processing time:

| Issuance time | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 10 working days | GEL 60 |
| 5 working days | GEL 80 |
| 3 working days | GEL 100 |
| 2 working days | GEL 120 |
| Same day | GEL 150 |

## Legal sources

This guide is based on the **Law of Georgia on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government of Georgia Resolution No. 520 of 1 September 2014** on the procedure for reviewing and deciding on the issuance of residence permits in Georgia. Official versions are published by the **Legislative Herald of Georgia (Matsne)**. Counting rules, grounds for loss, fees and processing times are periodically updated, so confirm the current requirements with the **Public Service Development Agency** before applying.
