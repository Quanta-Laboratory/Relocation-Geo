Georgia has become one of the most popular places in the region to study medicine, especially for students from India, the wider region and beyond. The draw is a combination of English-taught programmes, comparatively low tuition, and degrees that — from the right universities — are internationally listed. The essential rule for anyone considering it: recognition for *practising* medicine is decided by your home country, not by the university's marketing.

## The programme

The medical degree is a six-year MD (frequently advertised as MBBS), usually including a final-year clinical internship. Several universities run the programme entirely in English specifically for international students, and Georgia hosts well-established medical schools in Tbilisi and other cities. Teaching follows the Bologna structure, and the qualifying degree leads toward licensing exams rather than automatic registration.

## Cost

Medical tuition is a major part of Georgia's appeal. Fees are commonly in the range of about USD 4,000–8,000 per year depending on the university, with some private universities at the higher end. Including accommodation, food and the mandatory health insurance, students often budget in the region of USD 9,000–13,000 per year. These are indicative figures — confirm the current tuition and any fees directly with the university before committing.

## Accreditation and recognition — read this carefully

Two layers of recognition matter. Inside Georgia, the university must hold **NCEQE authorisation** and the medical programme must be **accredited**. Internationally, leading Georgian medical universities are listed in the **World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS)**, which many countries' licensing bodies treat as a baseline requirement.

What a Georgian degree does *not* do is grant an automatic right to practise abroad. Whether you can be licensed in India, the EU, the US, the Gulf or anywhere else depends on that country's medical board and its examinations — for example India's screening/licensing exam administered by the National Medical Commission, or the ECFMG pathway in the United States, which relies on WDOMS listing. Always check your home country's current rules before you enrol.

## How to choose safely

Before paying anything, verify three things: the university holds NCEQE authorisation; your specific medical programme is accredited; and the university is listed in WDOMS. Then cross-check your destination country's medical-board requirements. Treat agent promises of "guaranteed" recognition or licensing with caution — the only authorities that decide licensing are the universities' regulators and your home medical board.

## Entry, stay and insurance

Medical students follow the same immigration path as other students — the D3 visa and a study residence permit, covered in the [student visa and residence guide](https://relocation.ge/en/student-visa-and-residence-georgia/) — and must hold the [health insurance](https://relocation.ge/en/health-insurance-in-georgia-for-foreigners/) required to enter Georgia.
