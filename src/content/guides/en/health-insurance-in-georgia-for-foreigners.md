Georgia introduced a mandatory entry-insurance rule in 2026, so health cover now matters both for short-term visitors and for people relocating long term. This guide separates the two: the insurance you must hold to enter the country, and how healthcare works once you live here.

## Mandatory insurance to enter Georgia

From **1 January 2026**, anyone travelling to Georgia as a tourist must have **valid health and accident insurance**. This requirement applies to **all foreign visitors**, regardless of their nationality or whether they arrive by **air, land or sea**.

Your insurance must be valid **for your entire stay in Georgia**, from the day you enter until the day you leave. The maximum policy period recognised for this purpose is **one year**.

The policy may be issued by **either a Georgian or a foreign insurance company**. You can present it **electronically or on paper**, and it must be **in either Georgian or English**.

To be accepted, the insurance must provide at least the following minimum cover:

| Requirement         | Standard set by legislation                                       |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Minimum sum insured | At least GEL 30,000                                               |
| Cover               | Medical expenses and the consequences of accidents                |
| Validity            | Entire period of stay, from entry to departure (maximum one year) |
| Format              | Paper or electronic                                               |
| Language            | Georgian or English                                               |
| Insurer             | Georgian or foreign insurance company                             |

## Minimum insurance coverage

Your insurance policy must provide **at least GEL 30,000** in total coverage. This is the maximum amount the insurer may pay under the policy.

The insurance must cover **both medical treatment and accidents**. There is no legal requirement to allocate separate amounts for different types of medical care (such as outpatient or inpatient treatment) — only the **minimum total coverage of GEL 30,000** is required.

## What information must the policy include

The insurance policy or certificate should clearly state:

- the name of the insured person;
- the name of the insurance company;
- that the policy is valid in **Georgia**;
- what risks are covered (health and accident insurance);
- the total coverage amount;
- the policy start and end dates; 
- the insurance premium and payment details.

## Who does not need this insurance

The mandatory insurance requirement **does not apply** to:

- holders of a [diplomatic](/en/diplomatic-visa-georgia) or [special](/en/special-visa-georgia) visa;
- holders of a diplomatic, official, service or special passport;
- accredited staff of diplomatic missions, consulates and international organisations in Georgia, as well as their accredited family members;
- persons who are exempt under an **international treaty or agreement** binding on Georgia; and
- drivers engaged in international road freight and passenger transport.

These exemptions relate to a requirement that applies to **tourists entering Georgia**. Persons entering under another legal status, such as **holders of a valid Georgian residence permit**, are generally **not entering as tourists**, so this requirement does not apply to them.

## At the border

When you enter Georgia, border officers may ask you to show proof that you have the required insurance. They may also check that the policy meets the minimum requirements.

If you cannot present a valid policy, **you may be refused entry**.

To avoid delays, keep your insurance easily accessible, either as a **printed copy** or **on your phone**, and make sure it is valid for **your entire stay in Georgia**.

## Healthcare once you live in Georgia

If you are planning to live in Georgia, it is helpful to understand how the healthcare system works. For an overview, see [health in Georgia](/en/health-in-georgia); For practical information on finding doctors, visiting clinics, buying medicines and using the **112 emergency service**, see  [healthcare in Georgia](/en/healthcare-in-georgia). 

Georgia has a **Universal Healthcare Program**, but it is designed primarily for **Georgian citizens**. Foreign nationals who obtain a **Georgian residence permit** can register for the programme, but the level of coverage is usually limited. Free or heavily subsidised healthcare is mainly available to certain groups, such as **low-income residents, pensioners and large families**. Most foreign residents do not qualify for these categories.

Persons who have been granted **refugee, humanitarian or stateless status** in Georgia are covered on the same terms as Georgian citizens.

## Private health insurance

Because public healthcare provides only limited benefits for most foreign residents, many choose to take out **private health insurance**.

Georgia has a well-developed private healthcare sector, with modern clinics available in **Tbilisi, Batumi** and other major cities. Health insurance is also generally **more affordable than in many European countries**.

## Paying for healthcare yourself

If you do not have health insurance, you will generally need to pay the full cost of medical treatment yourself.

Routine healthcare in Georgia is often more affordable than in many Western European or North American countries, but emergency treatment, surgery or a prolonged hospital stay can still be expensive. Having appropriate health insurance helps protect you from unexpected medical costs and is now also a mandatory requirement for tourists entering Georgia.
