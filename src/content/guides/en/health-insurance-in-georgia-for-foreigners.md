Georgia introduced a mandatory entry-insurance rule in 2026, so health cover now matters both for short-term visitors and for people relocating long term. This guide separates the two: the insurance you must hold to enter the country, and how healthcare works once you live here.

## Mandatory insurance to enter Georgia

From 1 January 2026, a tourist entering Georgia must hold valid health and accident insurance. The requirement is set by Government of Georgia Decree No 602 of 26 December 2025, adopted under Article 50(1)(v) of the Law of Georgia on Tourism, and it establishes the minimum standard and conditions for this insurance. It applies regardless of nationality or the method of entry — air, land or sea.

The cover must run for the entire period from entry to departure — the decree caps the insured period at one year. The policy may be issued by a Georgian or a foreign insurance company, and it can be presented in paper or electronic form, in Georgian or English.

The core conditions the policy must meet are:

| Requirement | Standard set by Decree No 602 |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum sum insured | At least GEL 30,000 |
| Cover | Medical expenses and the consequences of accidents |
| Validity | Entire period of stay, from entry to departure (maximum one year) |
| Format | Paper or electronic |
| Language | Georgian or English |
| Insurer | Georgian or foreign insurance company |

## How much cover is required

The decree sets a single minimum: the policy's sum insured (the maximum limit the insurer is liable for) must be at least GEL 30,000. There are no separate statutory sub-limits for outpatient versus inpatient treatment — the GEL 30,000 figure is the legal minimum total. The cover must extend to both medical expenses and the consequences of accidents.

## What the policy must contain

Decree No 602 specifies the information the insurance document must show:

- the insured person and the insurer;
- the coverage territory (Georgia);
- the insured risks;
- the coverage amount (limit);
- the start and end dates of cover;
- the premium and payment terms.

## Who is exempt

Decree No 602 lists the categories it does not apply to:

- holders of a [diplomatic](/en/diplomatic-visa-georgia) or [special](/en/special-visa-georgia) visa;
- holders of a diplomatic, official, service or special passport;
- accredited staff of diplomatic missions, consular offices and international-organisation representations (and equivalent bodies), together with their accredited family members;
- persons whose entry to Georgia is provided for by Georgia's international agreements or treaties;
- drivers engaged in international road freight and passenger transport.

The rule is framed around the "tourist entering Georgia," with the insured period defined as entry to departure. Ordinary residence-permit holders are not named in this exemption list, so anyone relying on residence status to skip the policy should confirm their position against the current rule rather than assume an exemption.

## At the border

When you enter Georgia, an officer of the Patrol Police Department of the Ministry of Internal Affairs is authorised to check that you hold the required policy at the border crossing point, and to verify that it meets the decree's conditions. Entry may be refused if a valid policy is not presented. Keep proof accessible — a printout or a file on your phone — covering your full stay.

## Healthcare once you live in Georgia

For an overview of the system, see [health in Georgia](/en/health-in-georgia); for how care actually works day to day — clinics, pharmacies and the 112 emergency line — see [healthcare in Georgia](/en/healthcare-in-georgia). Georgia runs a state Universal Healthcare Program, but it is built mainly around Georgian citizens. A foreigner who obtains a residence permit can register, yet genuinely free services are generally reserved for vulnerable categories such as low-income residents, pensioners and large families. Most foreign residents do not fall into these groups and so receive only limited benefits. People with recognised stateless, refugee or humanitarian status are covered on the same terms as citizens.

## Private health insurance

Because state cover is limited for most foreigners, private health insurance is the common choice for residents. The market is well developed, premiums are relatively low by European standards, and private clinics are widely available in Tbilisi, Batumi and other cities. When comparing policies, check the annual limit, whether outpatient and dental care are included, the network of partner clinics, and how reimbursement or direct billing works.

## Paying out of pocket

Without insurance, a foreigner pays the full cost of any treatment directly. Routine care in Georgia is inexpensive compared with Western Europe or North America, but a serious hospitalisation can still be costly — one of the reasons the entry-insurance requirement now exists.

## Legal sources

The entry-insurance rule is based on the **Law of Georgia on Tourism** and **Government of Georgia Decree No 602 of 26 December 2025**, "On the Approval of the Rules and Conditions of Mandatory Health and Accident Insurance for Tourists Entering Georgia". Official texts are published by the Legislative Herald of Georgia (Matsne). Requirements and figures are periodically updated, so confirm the current rule before you travel.
