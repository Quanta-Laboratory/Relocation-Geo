A trademark protects the name, logo or other sign that identifies your goods or services. In Georgia, rights come from **registration with Sakpatenti**, the National Intellectual Property Center. Company registration and domain names do **not** give trademark rights.

This guide covers the national procedure, official fees, timelines, foreign applicants and international protection. If you have not set up your business yet, see **[Registering a Company in Georgia](/en/registering-a-company-in-georgia)** or **[Register as an Individual Entrepreneur Remotely](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely)**.

## At a glance

| | Standard route | Accelerated route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to decision | About 1 year including opposition | About 7 working days (opposition still possible for 3 months) |
| Official fees, 1 class | About USD 420 | About USD 840 |
| Each extra class | + USD 50 | + USD 300 |
| Electronic filing discount | 20% | 20% |
| Protection | 10 years, renewable | 10 years, renewable |

Fees are fixed in **US dollars** and paid in **lari** at the equivalent rate.

## What can be registered

A trademark can be a word, logo, combination of words and images, a three-dimensional shape, a colour combination or another sign that distinguishes your goods or services from others'.

Sakpatenti refuses a mark on **absolute grounds** (Article 4) if it:

- is not distinctive, or only describes the goods (for example, "Fresh Bread" for bread);
- has become the generic name for the product;
- is misleading about the nature, quality or origin of the goods;
- is contrary to public order or morality;
- uses state symbols, flags or official emblems without permission;
- is a shape that is purely functional or gives the product its essential value.

A descriptive mark can still be registered if it has acquired distinctiveness through use.

It also refuses a mark on **relative grounds** (Article 5) if it is identical or confusingly similar to an earlier mark for the same or similar goods. **Well-known marks** are protected in Georgia even without registration.

## Before you file

1. **Search the register.** Check for identical and similar marks in Sakpatenti's database for your classes. Sakpatenti also offers a paid preliminary search.
2. **Choose your classes.** Goods and services are grouped under the international **Nice Classification** (45 classes). The classes you list define what is protected, and you **cannot add classes after filing**; extra coverage needs a new application.
3. **Prepare the materials.** You need an image of the mark, the applicant's name and legal address, the list of goods and services by class, the representative's details if you use one, and a signature (Article 9(5)).

## The procedure

The application is filed **in Georgian** (Article 9(2)). The list of goods may be filed in another language if a Georgian translation follows within **one month** (15 days from 1 January 2027).

| Step | What happens | Legal deadline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Filing | Application and formal examination fee submitted to Sakpatenti | — |
| 2. Formal examination | Sakpatenti checks completeness and sets the filing date | Within 1 month of filing (Art. 13) |
| 3. Substantive examination | Sakpatenti checks registrability and conflicts with earlier marks | Within 6 months of formal examination (Art. 14) |
| 4. Publication | The mark is published in the Official Bulletin of Industrial Property | Within 1 month of approval (Art. 15) |
| 5. Opposition | Third parties can object | 3 months from publication (Art. 16) |
| 6. Registration | Certificate issued and mark entered in the register | After the opposition period or a final decision (Art. 17) |

### Accelerated procedure

You can request the **accelerated procedure** when filing or within 3 months of the acceptance notice (Article 15¹). Sakpatenti decides on registrability within **7 working days** once the full documents and fees are in. Third parties can still challenge the registration within **3 months of publication**.

### Oppositions and appeals

An opposition is filed with Sakpatenti's **Chamber of Appeals** within 3 months of publication. If the opponent relies on a mark registered more than 5 years earlier, the applicant can ask them to prove genuine use of it in Georgia.

An applicant can appeal a refusal to the Chamber of Appeals within 3 months. The Chamber's decision can be challenged in court. From **1 January 2027**, refusals and registration decisions can also be taken **directly to court** within 3 months of official notification.

## Official fees

Sakpatenti's fees for a standard trademark (US dollar equivalent, paid in lari):

| Item | 1 class | Each extra class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal examination | USD 90 | USD 50 |
| Substantive examination | USD 120 | — |
| Publication | USD 60 | — |
| Registration, certificate, 10 years' protection | USD 150 | — |
| **Total, standard route** | **about USD 420** | **+ USD 50** |
| Accelerated formal examination | USD 200 | USD 100 |
| Accelerated substantive examination | USD 300 | USD 100 |
| Accelerated registration, publication and certificate | USD 340 | USD 100 |
| **Total, accelerated route** | **about USD 840** | **+ USD 300** |
| Renewal for 10 years | USD 300 | USD 50 |
| Appeal to the Chamber of Appeals | USD 200 | — |
| Convention priority claim | USD 30 | — |

**Electronic filing reduces the fees by 20%**, including the certificate fee, so the standard route for one class costs about USD 336 online.

Since **26 October 2026**, fees are paid by the unique invoice number generated in Sakpatenti's electronic case system, through internet or mobile banking, a payment terminal or a bank. Fees paid by the old treasury code after that date are refunded on request.

Collective and certification marks have their own, higher fees.

## Foreign applicants

The Law on Trademarks lets any natural or legal person apply, **directly or through a representative**. In practice:

- A **non-resident** without a Georgian residence permit or Georgian company usually files through a **patent attorney** registered with Sakpatenti, who handles the Georgian-language filing and correspondence.
- An alternative is to **register a Georgian company or individual entrepreneur** first and file in its name. The trademark then belongs to that entity.
- Documents issued abroad, such as a power of attorney or priority document, may need an **apostille** and a Georgian translation.

## Priority and international protection

A Georgian registration protects your mark **only in Georgia**. To extend protection:

- **Paris Convention priority.** If you filed the same mark abroad within the last **6 months**, you can claim that filing date in Georgia (fee USD 30).
- **Madrid System.** Georgia is a member, so a single international application through WIPO can cover Georgia and other countries. The Georgian designation costs **CHF 314** for one class and **CHF 115** per extra class for 10 years. Sakpatenti can also act as the office of origin for Georgian applicants (USD 60 certification fee).
- **National filings** in each country remain possible.

## After registration

- **Term.** Protection runs for **10 years from registration** (Article 20) and can be renewed for further 10-year periods without limit.
- **Renewal.** File in the last year of protection. There is a **6-month grace period** after expiry; after that the registration is cancelled with no right to restore it.
- **Use it.** A court can cancel a registration that has **not been used in Georgia for 5 consecutive years** (Article 27), unless the non-use was beyond the owner's control.
- **Licences and assignments.** Record licence agreements and changes of owner in the register (USD 90 per mark for an agreement, USD 60 for other changes).
- **Enforcement.** The owner can stop others from using identical or confusingly similar signs for similar goods, through Sakpatenti, the courts and customs.

## Changes from 1 January 2027

Amendments adopted in October 2026 take effect on **1 January 2027**:

- refusals and registration decisions can be challenged **directly in court**, not only through the Chamber of Appeals;
- the Georgian translation of a foreign-language goods list is due within **15 calendar days** instead of one month;
- non-use and proof-of-use rules refer to **genuine use in Georgia**;
- the reputation ground is widened to protect well-known marks against unfair use on different goods.

Fees and procedures can change. Check the current fee table and requirements with **Sakpatenti** before filing.
