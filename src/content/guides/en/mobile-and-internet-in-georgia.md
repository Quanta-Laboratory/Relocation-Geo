Staying connected in Georgia is easy, fast and cheap — one of the quiet advantages for remote workers, students and new arrivals. Getting set up usually takes minutes, and prices are low by international standards.

## Getting a SIM

Three operators dominate the market: **Magti, Silknet and Cellfie**. You can buy a **prepaid SIM with just your passport** at the airport on arrival, at operator branches, or in many shops and kiosks. Activation is quick, and you can top up online or in shops. For a longer stay, compare the operators' current data bundles, as plans and promotions change often.

## Mobile data and coverage

**Mobile data is inexpensive** and bundles are generous. **4G coverage is strong in cities and along main routes**, with service thinning in remote mountain areas. For most people a prepaid data plan is more than enough for work, navigation and streaming.

## eSIM

The main operators increasingly **support eSIM**, which is handy if your phone is compatible — you can sometimes set up a line without a physical SIM. Availability and plans vary, so confirm directly with the operator.

## Home internet

**Fixed fibre broadband is widely available** in cities, mainly through Magti and Silknet, at low monthly prices. Connecting an apartment is usually straightforward; if you rent, the property may already have a line you can transfer or reactivate — the [renting a home guide](https://relocation.ge/en/renting-a-home-in-georgia/) covers the lease side. Reliable, fast home internet is part of why Georgia works well as a base for [digital nomads](https://relocation.ge/en/georgia-for-digital-nomads/).

## Regulation and plans

The sector is overseen by the **Georgian National Communications Commission (ComCom)**, while operators set their own retail tariffs. Because promotions and bundle sizes change frequently, treat any specific plan you read about as a snapshot and check the operator's current offer when you buy.
