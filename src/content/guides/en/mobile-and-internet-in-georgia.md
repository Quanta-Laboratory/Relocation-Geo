Staying connected in Georgia is easy, fast and cheap — one of the quiet advantages for remote workers, students and new arrivals. Getting set up usually takes minutes, and prices are low by international standards.

## Getting a SIM

Three operators dominate the market: **Magti, Silknet and Cellfie**. You can buy a **prepaid SIM with typically using your passport** at the airport on arrival, at operator branches, or in many shops and kiosks. Activation is quick, and you can top up online or in shops. For a longer stay, compare the operators' current data bundles, as plans and promotions change often. For a side-by-side comparison of the three — coverage, eSIM, home internet and how to choose — see **[mobile operators in Georgia](/en/mobile-operators-georgia)**.

## Mobile data and coverage

**Mobile data is inexpensive** and bundles are generous. **4G coverage is strong in cities and along main routes**, with service thinning in remote mountain areas. For most people a prepaid data plan is more than enough for work, navigation and streaming.

## eSIM

All three major operators currently offer eSIM for compatible devices— you can sometimes set up a line without a physical SIM. Availability and plans vary, so confirm directly with the operator. An eSIM is also the usual answer for people who registered a business in Georgia **before** arriving and now need a local number to operate it — see the section below.

## The number is your key to government and banking

Georgian government and financial services authenticate you with a **one-time code sent by SMS to a Georgian number**. Many government and banking services are designed to authenticate users through SMS sent to a Georgian mobile number. In practice, relying solely on a foreign number can make it difficult or impossible to use certain online services.

In practice, a local number is what gates:

- the **Revenue Service (rs.ge)** — activating your taxpayer account, applying for [Small Business Status](/en/small-business-status-georgia), and filing the **declaration**;
- **Public Service Hall** and government e-services;
- your **bank account** — authentication codes, transaction confirmations and security notifications.

The trap is specific to people who set things up remotely. You can [register an Individual Entrepreneur from abroad](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely) through a Power of Attorney, and a representative can complete that registration for you. Although a representative can complete the registration process, they cannot permanently operate your personal banking or tax accounts on your behalf and each of them wants a Georgian number.

So if you are arriving to finish a business setup, get the SIM or eSIM **first**, before the bank and before the tax portal. It takes minutes and unblocks everything downstream.

If you later change your Georgian mobile number, remember to update it with your bank, the Revenue Service and any other government services that use SMS authentication.
## Home internet

**Fixed fibre broadband is widely available** in cities, mainly through Magti and Silknet, at low monthly prices. Connecting an apartment is usually straightforward; if you rent, the property may already have a line you can transfer or reactivate — the [renting a home guide](https://relocation.ge/en/renting-a-home-in-georgia/) covers the lease side. Reliable, fast home internet is part of why Georgia works well as a base for [digital nomads](https://relocation.ge/en/georgia-for-digital-nomads/).

## Regulation and plans

The sector is overseen by the **Georgian National Communications Commission (ComCom)**, while operators set their own retail tariffs. Because promotions and bundle sizes change frequently, treat any specific plan you read about as a snapshot and check the operator's current offer when you buy.
