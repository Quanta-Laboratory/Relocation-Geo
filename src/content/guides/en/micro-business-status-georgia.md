**Micro Business Status** is one of Georgia's **[special tax regimes](/en/special-tax-regimes-georgia)** and offers the most favourable treatment for very small activity. It is designed for **individuals who carry out small-scale economic activity personally, without hired labour** — so it effectively operates as a **one-person business**.

For freelancers, artisans, independent creators or people testing a business idea, the regime provides a simple way to operate legally with **minimal bureaucracy and virtually no tax burden**. Like any status, it works within clearly defined limits.

## Who may obtain it

Micro Business Status may be granted to a **natural person who is registered as a taxpayer**. Registration as an **Individual Entrepreneur is not mandatory** to obtain the status.

To qualify, a person must generally meet two conditions:

- **No hired labour.** The regime is for persons who perform economic activity independently.
- **Turnover limit.** Expected **total gross income during the calendar year must not exceed GEL 30,000**, except where the law treats certain activities or income types differently.

In essence the regime is built around three principles: **personal work**, **small scale**, and **limited turnover**.

## The GEL 30,000 rule: important, but not absolute

As a general rule, a holder stays within the regime as long as **total gross income from qualifying economic activity during the year does not exceed GEL 30,000**. The law provides two exceptions:

- **Certain government-defined activities** are **not subject to the GEL 30,000 cap**. Income from these activities can still fall under the micro business regime (see the list at the end of this guide).
- **Certain income types are not treated as micro business income.** These amounts are **not counted** toward the GEL 30,000 limit **but are taxed under the ordinary rules**.

So a person with the status may simultaneously receive income covered by the micro regime, income that does not affect the threshold but is taxed normally, and income or activities that could even lead to cancellation. Micro Business Status does **not** make all income tax-free — the exemption applies only to the specific categories defined by law.

## What income is covered

Covered income generally consists of income from **qualifying economic activity**:

- income from economic activity within the **GEL 30,000 threshold**; and
- income from certain government-defined activities for which the threshold does not apply.

Such income is subject to the micro business regime and is **exempt from personal income tax (0%)**, provided the activity is not among those excluded by law and the income is not salary income.

| Business size | Tax system | Tax rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micro business | up to GEL 30,000 | 0% |
| Small business | up to GEL 500,000 | 1% |
| Standard taxation | above that | 20% |

For the standard rate and how income is sourced, see **[Personal Income Tax in Georgia](/en/personal-income-tax-georgia)**; whether Georgia taxes you at all depends on your **[tax residency](/en/tax-residency-georgia)**.

## Prohibited activities

Micro Business Status is **not available for every activity**. If a person engages in an activity defined by the Government as prohibited for micro business, the tax authority may refuse or cancel the status. Earning under GEL 30,000 and working alone is not enough on its own — the **nature of the activity must also be compatible** with the regime.

The prohibited activities are:

1. activities that require **licensing or a permit**, except the taxi (M1 category) passenger-transport permit for the capital under the Law "On Licenses and Permits";
2. activities from which annual income may **exceed GEL 30,000**;
3. **foreign-exchange operations**;
4. **medical** activity (except a village doctor and nurse/paramedic in the state programme), **architectural**, **advocacy or notarial**, **auditing**, and **consulting (including tax consulting)** activity;
5. **gambling business**;
6. **trade** — but this does not apply where purchased goods are **processed and supplied**.

## A VAT payer cannot hold the status

A person registered as a **VAT payer** cannot obtain Micro Business Status. This reflects the purpose of the regime: it is intended for very small-scale activity, whereas VAT registration generally indicates a larger scale.

## How the status is granted

A person applies to the tax authority. If already registered as a taxpayer, they submit an application for the status; if not yet registered, they may apply simultaneously for **tax registration and micro business status**.

If approved, the tax authority issues a **micro business certificate**. The status becomes effective **from the date indicated in the application**, provided that date is not earlier than the date the application was registered with the tax authority.

## Reporting and documentation

The regime simplifies taxation but does not remove compliance entirely.

- A holder **pays no current tax during the year** but must file an **annual personal income tax declaration by 1 April** of the following year.
- A holder may issue a **primary tax document** for goods or services if the buyer requests it, indicating their **micro business status** and the **certificate number**.
- Tax documents must be kept for **3 years** from the end of the relevant tax year.
- Micro businesses are generally **exempt from cash-register requirements**, though the Government may set exceptions for certain activities or municipal areas.
- Where the person receives services from a **non-resident**, they may still be liable for **VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism**, even though they generally do not act as a withholding agent.

## When the status is cancelled

Micro Business Status may be cancelled during the year for reasons including:

- voluntary cancellation requested by the taxpayer;
- use of hired labour;
- performance of a prohibited activity;
- mandatory VAT registration;
- exceeding the relevant income threshold;
- exceeding the permitted inventory level;
- death of the individual.

If the status is cancelled, the individual may apply again **from the following tax year**; if granted again, a **new certificate with a new registration number** is issued.

## What happens if the business grows

If annual income exceeds **GEL 30,000**, the individual may transition to **[Small Business Status](/en/small-business-status-georgia)**, under which income up to **GEL 500,000** per year is taxed at **1%** of turnover. This creates a natural progression — start under the 0% micro regime and grow gradually without facing heavy taxation early on. For a specific listed activity such as hairdressing or car repair, see **[Fixed Taxpayer Status](/en/fixed-taxpayer-status-georgia)** instead.

## Activities exempt from the GEL 30,000 limit

The following activities are **not subject to the GEL 30,000 cap**, provided the person independently carries out the activity, uses no hired labour, and personally produces and supplies the goods or services to the final consumer on a retail basis:

1. cultivation of agricultural products using tractors or combines;
2. production of carpets and carpet-type products;
3. production of pullovers and similar products;
4. production of outerwear (except demonstration and display of models);
5. production of underwear;
6. production of headwear;
7. production of other clothing and accessories;
8. production of various wooden products;
9. production of wooden hangers for clothing and headwear;
10. production of wooden household items and kitchen utensils;
11. production of porcelain and faience household goods;
12. production of household utensils and inventory;
13. production of musical instruments;
14. production of accordions and similar instruments, including harmonicas;
15. production of wind musical instruments;
16. production of brooms and brushes;
17. repair of household goods and personal-use items;
18. repair of household goods and personal-use items carried out independently of production or trade of those items;
19. repair of footwear and leather products;
20. repair of household electronic products;
21. repair of watches and jewellery;
22. alteration and repair of clothing;
23. repair of household goods and personal-use items;
24. washing and processing of linen and other textile products;
25. services related to running a household.

## Income always taxed under the ordinary rules

The following income is **not** taxed under the special regime and is **not** counted toward the GEL 30,000 limit:

1. income from leasing or renting out real estate;
2. income from granting a loan;
3. winnings from gambling;
4. income received as a gift;
5. excess income from selling real estate, motor vehicles or securities;
6. income in the form of inherited property;
7. income in the form of dividends;
8. income in the form of interest;
9. income in the form of royalties;
10. income from forgiveness of debt.
