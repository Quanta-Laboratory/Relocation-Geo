The **Special Residence Permit** is a distinct residence category available only in **specific circumstances defined by Georgian legislation**. Depending on the legal ground, it may be granted in the **interests of the State**, to provide **protection for victims of human trafficking**, under the statutory route provided by **Article 60** of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons, or to **foreign compatriots** living abroad.

For an overview of all residence categories, see the **[Guide to Residence Permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.

## Who is eligible

A Special Residence Permit may be granted in four distinct cases.

**A. At the initiative of a member of the Government of Georgia.** 
A residence permit may be issued to a foreigner **upon the written initiative of a member of the Government of Georgia**.

**B. Victims of human trafficking.** 
A residence permit may be issued to a **victim or injured party of the crime of human trafficking**, based on a **motion from the institution providing services to victims of trafficking or from the competent procedural authority**.

**C. Persons provided for in Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons**
A foreigner may obtain a Special Residence Permit if they previously held the **right of temporary stay in Georgia under Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons**. Under Article 60:
- The **Ministry of Internal Affairs** may grant a foreigner the **right of temporary stay in Georgia** when the foreigner **cannot be expelled from Georgia due to specific legal or factual circumstances**, including:
    - expulsion is impossible due to legal restrictions provided by law;
    - the circumstances related to expulsion have not changed;
    - the foreigner **cannot be identified**;
    - **no state agrees to accept the foreigner**;
    - other circumstances defined by law.
- In such cases, the foreigner is issued a **Temporary Identification Card** confirming the right of temporary stay.
- The **right of temporary stay is granted for 1 year**, and may be **extended for additional one-year periods**.
- During this period, the Ministry of Internal Affairs periodically reviews whether the circumstances preventing expulsion still exist.
- After **5 years from the issuance of the Temporary Identification Card**, the foreigner **may apply to the Public Service Development Agency for a Special Residence Permit**.
- A **Special Residence Permit issued on this basis is granted for 1 year** and may be **extended annually upon request of the Ministry of Internal Affairs**.

**D. Persons holding the status of a compatriot living abroad.** 
A **citizen of another state who holds the status of a compatriot living abroad** may apply for a Special Residence Permit.

## Duration and validity

The duration of a **Special Residence Permit** depends on the legal ground on which it is granted.

For persons covered by **Article 60** of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons (persons who cannot be removed from Georgia), the permit is **issued for one year**. It may be **extended for further one-year periods** upon the application of the authorised body of the Ministry of Internal Affairs, provided the statutory conditions continue to be met. The underlying **right of temporary stay** under Article 60 is likewise granted for **one year at a time** and is reviewed periodically by the Ministry of Internal Affairs.

For Special Residence Permits granted **on the initiative of a member of the Government, to victims of human trafficking, or to foreign compatriots living abroad**, the legislation does not prescribe a fixed period of validity. The permit is issued in accordance with the applicable legal provisions and the circumstances of the particular case.

## Application

Applications are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia**. The document set depends on which situation applies.

**Residence permit issued at the initiative of a Government member:**

- the application;
- a copy of the foreigner's passport (travel document);
- the written request of a member of the Government of Georgia;
- a document confirming lawful stay in Georgia;
- a colour photograph, 3x4 cm, in electronic format.

**Victims of human trafficking:**

- a motion submitted by the institution providing services to victims of trafficking or by the competent procedural authority.

**Persons provided for in Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons:**

- the application;
- a copy of the foreigner's passport (if available);
- a copy of a valid temporary identification card;
- a certificate from the competent authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs confirming lawful temporary stay in Georgia for the five years before applying;
- a colour photograph, 3x4 cm, in electronic format;
- the receipt confirming payment of the service fee.

**Foreign nationals holding the status of a compatriot living abroad:**

- the application;
- a copy of the foreigner's passport;
- a document confirming lawful stay in Georgia;
- a document confirming the status of a compatriot living abroad;
- a colour photograph, 3x4 cm, in electronic format.

**Extension of a Special Residence Permit (Article 60 cases):**

- the application;
- a copy of the foreigner's passport (if available);
- a copy of a valid temporary identification card;
- a request of the competent authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs regarding extension of the permit;
- a colour photograph, 3x4 cm, in electronic format;
- the receipt confirming payment of the service fee.

## Processing time and fees

A **Special Residence Permit** is generally **issued within 10 calendar days** from the submission of the application. In **most cases**, **no state service fee is payable**.

The exception is **cases under Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** (persons who cannot be removed from Georgia). For these applications, the standard residence permit fee schedule applies:

| Processing time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | GEL 300 |
| 20 calendar days | GEL 450 |
| 10 calendar days | GEL 600 |

## Georgian ID (residence) card

After obtaining the Special Residence Permit, a foreign national must apply for a **Georgian residence (ID) card** within one month. Applications may be submitted through the Public Service Hall or a territorial office of the Public Service Development Agency.

The fee depends on the processing time:

| Issuance time | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 10 working days | GEL 60 |
| 5 working days | GEL 80 |
| 3 working days | GEL 100 |
| 2 working days | GEL 120 |
| Same day | GEL 150 |

## How it differs from the ordinary permits

Unlike **[Work](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia)**, **[Investment](/en/investment-residence-permit-georgia)** or **[Family Reunification](/en/family-reunification-residence-permit-georgia)** residence permits, a **Special Residence Permit** is generally **not obtained through an ordinary immigration application based on employment, investment or family ties**. Instead, it is available only in specific situations defined by law, including **a written initiative of a member of the Government of Georgia**, **protection of victims of human trafficking**, **the statutory procedure for persons covered by Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons**, and **foreign compatriots living abroad**.

If you believe you may qualify under one of these categories, you should contact the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** or the competent authority responsible for the relevant procedure.

Human trafficking is a sensitive matter. If you or someone you know may be a victim of human trafficking, contact the competent authorities or an organisation providing assistance to victims. This guide provides **general information only**and does **not constitute legal advice**. Residence permit requirements, procedures and state fees may change over time.
