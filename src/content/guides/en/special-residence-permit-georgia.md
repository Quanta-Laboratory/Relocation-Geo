The **Special Residence Permit** is not a single eligibility test but a **discretionary and protective** category granted to a **foreigner in specific circumstances defined by Georgian legislation**. It covers cases involving state interest, **protection for trafficking victims**, a statutory route under **Article 60**, and **foreign compatriots** living abroad. This guide sets out the four situations it covers, who is eligible, the required documents, the duration and the fee position — checked against the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government Resolution No. 520**. For the wider framework, see the **[guide to residence permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.

## Who is eligible

A Special Residence Permit may be granted in four distinct cases.

**A. At the initiative of a member of the Government of Georgia.** A residence permit may be issued to a foreigner **upon the written initiative of a member of the Government of Georgia**.

**B. Victims of human trafficking.** A residence permit may be issued to a **victim or injured party of the crime of human trafficking**, based on a **motion from the institution providing services to victims of trafficking or from the competent procedural authority**.

**C. Persons provided for in Article 60 of the Law.** A foreigner may obtain a Special Residence Permit if they previously held the **right of temporary stay in Georgia under Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons**. Under Article 60:

- the **Ministry of Internal Affairs** may grant a foreigner the **right of temporary stay** when the foreigner **cannot be expelled from Georgia** due to specific legal or factual circumstances — for example, expulsion is impossible due to legal restrictions, the circumstances related to expulsion have not changed, the foreigner **cannot be identified**, **no state agrees to accept** them, or other circumstances defined by law;
- the foreigner is issued a **Temporary Identification Card** confirming the right of temporary stay;
- the **right of temporary stay is granted for one year** and may be **extended for further one-year periods**, with the Ministry periodically reviewing whether the circumstances preventing expulsion still exist;
- **five years** after the Temporary Identification Card is issued, the foreigner **may apply to the Public Service Development Agency for a Special Residence Permit**;
- a Special Residence Permit issued on this basis is **granted for one year** and may be **extended annually at the request of the Ministry of Internal Affairs**.

**D. Persons holding the status of a compatriot living abroad.** A **citizen of another state who holds the status of a compatriot living abroad** may apply for a Special Residence Permit.

## Duration and validity

The duration depends on the situation. For **Article 60** cases the permit is **granted for one year** and may be **extended annually** at the request of the Ministry of Internal Affairs. The underlying right of temporary stay under Article 60 is itself granted for one year at a time and reviewed periodically. For the other limbs, the permit is issued on the basis of the circumstances that triggered it and, where relevant, renewed while those circumstances persist.

## Required documents

Applications are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency of Georgia**. The document set depends on which situation applies.

**Residence permit issued at the initiative of a Government member:**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport** (travel document);
- the **written request of a member of the Government of Georgia**;
- a **document confirming lawful stay** in Georgia;
- a **colour photograph, 3x4**, in electronic format.

**Victims of human trafficking:**

- a **motion submitted by the institution providing services to victims of trafficking or by the competent procedural authority**.

**Persons provided for in Article 60 of the Law:**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport** (if available);
- a **copy of a valid temporary identification card**;
- a **certificate from the competent authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs** confirming lawful temporary stay in Georgia for the **five years** before applying;
- a **colour photograph, 3x4**, in electronic format;
- the **receipt** confirming payment of the service fee.

**Foreign nationals holding the status of a compatriot living abroad:**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport**;
- a **document confirming lawful stay** in Georgia;
- a **document confirming the status of a compatriot living abroad**;
- a **colour photograph, 3x4**, in electronic format.

**Extension of a Special Residence Permit (Article 60 cases):**

- the **application**;
- a **copy of the foreigner's passport** (if available);
- a **copy of a valid temporary identification card**;
- a **request of the competent authority of the Ministry of Internal Affairs** regarding extension of the permit;
- a **colour photograph, 3x4**, in electronic format;
- the **receipt** confirming payment of the service fee.

## Processing time and fees

The Special Residence Permit is normally **issued within 10 days** from the submission of the application, and in **most cases no service fee is charged**.

The **exception is Article 60 cases**, where the standard fee schedule applies:

| Processing time | State fee |
| --- | --- |
| 30 calendar days | GEL 300 |
| 20 calendar days | GEL 450 |
| 10 calendar days | GEL 600 |

## Georgian ID (residence) card

After the permit is granted, a foreign national may apply for a **Georgian ID (residence) card** through the Public Service Hall or the Public Registry. The fee depends on the processing time:

| Issuance time | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 10 working days | GEL 60 |
| 5 working days | GEL 80 |
| 3 working days | GEL 100 |
| 2 working days | GEL 120 |
| Same day | GEL 150 |

## How it differs from the ordinary permits

Unlike the **[work](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia)**, **[investment](/en/investment-residence-permit-georgia)** or **[family](/en/family-reunification-residence-permit-georgia)** permits, most limbs of the Special Residence Permit are **triggered by circumstances** — a Government member's initiative, identification as a trafficking victim, an Article 60 situation, or compatriot status — rather than by an open application you choose to make. If your situation may fall within one of these categories, approach the **Public Service Development Agency** or the relevant authority directly.

Human-trafficking protection is a sensitive matter. If you or someone you know may be affected, contact the relevant authorities and support services. This guide is **general information, not legal advice**, and the categories, issuance times and fees can change.

## Legal sources

This guide is based on the **Law of Georgia on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government of Georgia Resolution No. 520 of 1 September 2014** on the procedure for reviewing and deciding on the issuance of residence permits in Georgia. Official versions of the legislation are published by the **Legislative Herald of Georgia (Matsne)** at [matsne.gov.ge](https://www.matsne.gov.ge). Categories, thresholds, fees and processing times are periodically updated, so confirm the current position with the **Public Service Development Agency** before relying on a particular treatment.
