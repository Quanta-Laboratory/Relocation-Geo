The **Special Residence Permit** is not a single eligibility test but an umbrella for several **discretionary and protective** situations that do not fit the ordinary work, study, investment or family categories. It groups together cases initiated at the highest level of government, **protection for trafficking victims**, a statutory category under **Article 60**, and **foreign compatriots** abroad. This guide sets out the four situations it covers, the fast issuance, the fee position and how it differs from the standard permits — checked against the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and **Government Resolution No. 520**.

## The four situations it covers

The permit applies to four distinct groups:

**Government-initiated residence** — foreigners whose residence in Georgia is **initiated by a member of the Government of Georgia**.

**Trafficking victims** — **victims and affected persons of human trafficking**, as a protective measure giving them a lawful basis to remain.

**Article 60 persons** — foreigners covered by **Article 60 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons**.

**Compatriots abroad** — **foreign compatriots residing abroad** who hold a recognised connection to Georgia.

## Fast issuance

Reflecting its protective and discretionary character, the Special Residence Permit is normally **issued quickly — within about ten days** — rather than running through the longer review windows of the ordinary permits.

## The fee position

In **most cases there is no application fee** for the Special Residence Permit. The **exception is Article 60 cases**, which follow the **standard schedule** — **GEL 300** for a **30-day** decision, **GEL 450** for **20 days** and **GEL 600** for **10 days** — plus the separate **residence-card** fee. A residence (ID) card is issued in the usual way once the permit is granted.

## How it differs from the ordinary permits

Unlike the **[work](https://relocation.ge/en/work-residence-permit-georgia/)**, **[investment](https://relocation.ge/en/investment-residence-permit-georgia/)** or **[family](https://relocation.ge/en/family-reunification-residence-permit-georgia/)** permits, most limbs of the Special Residence Permit are **triggered by circumstances** — a Government member's initiative, identification as a trafficking victim, an Article 60 situation, or compatriot status — rather than by an open application you choose to make. If your situation may fall within one of these categories, approach the **Public Service Development Agency** or the relevant authority directly.

Human-trafficking protection is a sensitive matter. If you or someone you know may be affected, contact the relevant authorities and support services. This guide is **general information, not legal advice**, and the categories, issuance times and fees can change — confirm the current position with the **Public Service Development Agency** or an adviser before relying on a particular treatment.
