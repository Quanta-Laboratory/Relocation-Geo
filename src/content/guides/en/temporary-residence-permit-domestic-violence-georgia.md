Under Georgian law, foreign nationals recognised as victims of domestic violence or violence against women may qualify for a dedicated temporary residence permit. Unlike most residence permits, this route **waives certain general immigration requirements**, allowing eligible victims to apply even where they could not satisfy the ordinary residence permit conditions. This guide explains who may qualify, how victim status is established, which requirements are waived, and where to seek help, based on the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and the **Law on the Elimination of Violence against Women and/or Domestic Violence**.

## Who qualifies

The permit is available to a **foreign national recognised as a victim** of domestic violence or violence against women in accordance with the **Law on the Elimination of Violence against Women and/or Domestic Violence**.

Victim status is established under the procedures provided by that law and may be evidenced, where applicable, by:

- a **restraining order** or **protective order** issued in the victim's favour;
- **criminal proceedings** relating to the alleged domestic violence or violence against women; or
- documentation confirming that the person has received assistance from a **state shelter** or another authorised victim-support service.

The competent authorities will assess the evidence submitted in accordance with the applicable legislation.


## Why the normal conditions are waived

For most residence permits, an applicant must be **lawfully staying** in Georgia and submit the application within the prescribed time limits, including the usual **40-day pre-expiry rule**. These requirements **do not apply** to applications for a residence permit for victims of domestic violence or violence against women.

As a result, a foreign national is **not disqualified solely because they no longer satisfy the ordinary lawful-stay or application-timing requirements**. This special procedure is intended to ensure that immigration formalities do not prevent eligible victims from accessing legal protection.

## How applications are assessed

Applications for this residence permit are submitted to the Public Service Development Agency. 
The Public Service Development Agency determines whether the applicant meets the residence permit requirements based on the evidence provided and any information obtained from the competent authorities. Where necessary, additional documents or information may be requested during the examination of the application.

## Duration and renewal

A residence permit for victims of domestic violence or violence against women is **temporary**. The exact period of validity depends on the circumstances of the case and the applicable legislation. Where the legal grounds continue to exist, the permit may be renewed in accordance with Georgian law.

## Where to get help

If you are in **immediate danger, call 112**. Georgia operates **state shelters** and **support services** for victims of domestic violence and violence against women. Specialised NGOs and the competent state authorities can explain your legal options — including this residence permit where applicable — and assist you throughout the process.

The law provides a **dedicated residence permit route** for eligible victims, including exemptions from certain ordinary immigration requirements. If your immigration status has become irregular because of domestic violence or violence against women, you should still seek assistance from the competent authorities or a victim-support service.

This is a **sensitive topic**, and this guide provides **general information only** and does **not constitute legal advice**. The applicable legal requirements and administrative procedures may change over time, so you should confirm the current position with the **Public Service Development Agency**, a victim-support service, or a qualified lawyer. If you or someone you know is experiencing domestic violence, contacting a support service is a safe first step.
