Georgia maintains a dedicated **temporary residence permit for victims of domestic violence** and violence against women. Its defining feature is that it **removes the usual immigration barriers**: a victim whose status has lapsed, or who could not meet the standard timing rules, can still obtain a lawful basis to stay and access protection. This guide explains who qualifies, how victim status is shown, why the normal conditions are waived, and where to seek help — checked against the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and the **Law on the Elimination of Violence against Women and/or Domestic Violence**.

## Who qualifies

The permit is for a **foreigner recognised as a victim** of domestic violence or violence against women under the relevant law. Recognition is typically established through one of the law's protective mechanisms:

a **restraining order or protective order** issued in the victim's favour;

an **ongoing criminal prosecution** against the perpetrator; or

documented **use of a state shelter** or victim-support service.

## Why the normal conditions are waived

For most permits, an applicant must have been **lawfully staying** in Georgia and must file before specific deadlines — including the usual **40-day pre-expiry rule**. For this permit, **those conditions do not apply**. The waiver is deliberate: abusers often control a partner's documents and status, so a protection route that depended on having a clean immigration record would fail the people it is meant to help. A victim whose stay has become irregular can still apply.

## How victim status is evidenced

Eligibility turns on **victim status under the law**, evidenced through the mechanisms above — a protective or restraining order, the existence of a prosecution, or engagement with a state shelter or support service. Front-line services and the relevant authorities can help assemble what is needed.

## Where to get help

If you are in **immediate danger, call 112**. Georgia operates **state shelters** and a **support service** for victims of domestic violence, and specialised NGOs and state agencies can explain your options — including this permit — and help you apply. This permit exists to protect victims **regardless of immigration status**, so a lapsed permit should not stop you from seeking help.

This is a **sensitive topic**, and this guide is **general information, not legal advice**. Durations, evidentiary requirements and procedures can change, so confirm the current position with the **Public Service Development Agency**, a victim-support service or a lawyer. If you or someone you know is affected by domestic violence, reaching out to a support service is a safe first step.
