Georgia spent a decade building a reputation as a low-friction base for tech founders through regimes such as the [Virtual Zone Person](https://relocation.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-georgia/), the [International Company status](https://relocation.ge/en/international-company-status-georgia/) and the 1% [Small Business Status](https://relocation.ge/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely/). In **mid-2025 it went further** and built a dedicated framework aimed specifically at **innovative, investment-backed startups**. This guide maps the tools a founder in Georgia can actually use — the new statuses first, then the grants, accelerators and agencies around them — and points to the established regimes that may still fit better depending on the business. The substance below is checked against the **primary Georgian law**; programme figures that come only from agency announcements are flagged as such.

## The 2025 innovation reform in one paragraph

On **24 June 2025** Parliament passed **Law No 718**, amending the **Law of Georgia on Innovations** (published 27 June 2025, with the substantive provisions entering into force on the 90th day — late September 2025). The reform rebuilt Chapter II of the law around an **Innovations Registry** and created **three new statuses**: the **Innovative Startup**, the **Innovative SME**, and the **R&D Service Provider**. A parallel Tax Code amendment ties the tax treatment of these statuses to the Law on Innovations. The statuses are run by **Georgia's Innovation and Technology Agency (GITA)** as registrar, but the status itself is **granted by the Government of Georgia** on the recommendation of the **Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development**.

## Innovative Startup Status: the centrepiece

The Innovative Startup Status is the most generous of the three and the one most relocating founders will care about.

**Who qualifies.** The applicant must be a company registered in Georgia under the Law on Entrepreneurs that **creates an innovative product, develops an innovative process, or provides innovative services**. Crucially, it must have **secured outside funding** in the two years before applying — either at least **100,000 GEL of investment** from a registered **investment fund**, an **angel-investor network or one of its members**, or an **accelerator**; or at least **150,000 GEL of grant funding from GITA**. Funding routed through a foreign parent or subsidiary counts, provided that entity in turn received it from one of the approved sources. The company's **income and expenses must be substantially tied** to the funded activity, and it must have **no tax debt**, not be in the debtors' registry, face no insolvency proceedings and not be in liquidation.

**What it gives.** The relief runs in three phases over a maximum of **ten years**:

- **Years 1–3:** salaries the startup pays are **exempt from the 20% personal income tax**, up to a ceiling fixed by government decree — set at **10,000 GEL per month per employee**.
- **Years 4–6:** salaries are taxed at **5%** income tax, and the **profit (corporate) tax rate is 5%** (against the standard 15% on distributed profit).
- **Years 7–10:** salary income tax and profit tax both rise to **10%**.

**Staying in.** The status is granted for **one year** and renewed a year at a time, subject to milestones. For the first two years the company simply keeps meeting the entry conditions; to continue into **years 3–5** it must have raised at least **5 million GEL** in total investment by the end of year three (≥30% from the approved sources); and into **years 6–10**, at least **15 million GEL** by the end of year six (again ≥30% from approved sources). A startup must file proof of continued eligibility with GITA **30 days before** its current year expires.

**The hard limit.** You **cannot hold the Innovative Startup Status together with International Company Status or Virtual Zone Person status**. If a company already holding one of those is granted startup status, the older status is cancelled. So this is a choice, not a stack — see the comparison below.

## Innovative SME Status: for R&D-heavy companies

This status targets **established, profitable companies that invest in research**, not early-stage startups. It is open to a company that is a **Category III or IV enterprise** under the Law on Accounting, Reporting and Audit and whose **prior-year R&D spending was at least 5% of revenue and at least 100,000 GEL**, and that meets at least one further test: it **holds a patent or registered (deposited) software** to which the R&D is directed; it **buys R&D services** from a research institution or an R&D-Service-Provider-status company; or it **records its R&D costs** under the government-approved R&D Expenses Manual.

The benefit is built around **dividends**: when distributing the prior year's net profit, the company may **reduce the profit-tax base by three times its prior-year R&D expenses**, and it may use further support for **reinvested profit** as set by government decree. The status is granted **indefinitely**, with an annual compliance filing due by **30 April**.

## R&D Service Provider Status: a permanent low rate

The third status is for a company whose **main activity is research and experimental development** — specifically **NACE code 72.1**, "research and experimental development in natural sciences and engineering" — earning at least **80% of its revenue** from that activity, with clean financial standing. The reward is a **permanent 5% income-tax rate on salaries and a 5% profit-tax rate**. It is granted **once, indefinitely**, which makes Georgia attractive for setting up a regional R&D centre or research lab.

## How to apply and what it costs

For all three statuses the route is the same: the applicant files with **GITA**, which reviews the documents and forwards the application to the **Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development**; the **Government** then grants the status by decree. The status takes effect from the **first day of the month following** the decision. Registration in the Innovations Registry costs **200 GEL for an Innovative Startup** and **500 GEL** for an Innovative SME or R&D Service Provider (the 500 GEL fee also applies to investment funds, angel networks and accelerators that register in the system). The detailed procedure, document list and review timelines are set out in a **government resolution**, which is the document to check before filing, alongside the official **innovationstatus.gov.ge** portal.

## The funding and acceleration layer

The statuses assume a startup can attract investment or grants, and the reform formalised the players that count. An **investment fund** qualifies for the registry if it has put at least **1 million GEL** into innovative/tech startups over three years (holding ≤20% in each); an **angel-investor network** must unite at least **10 angels** who together invested at least **300,000 GEL** in at least five companies over two years (≤20% each); and an **accelerator** must run a **syllabus-based programme of 3–12 months** with workspace and qualified mentors.

In practice the central public actor is **GITA**, which runs grant and acceleration programmes. Its **Innovative Startup Acceleration Programme** has been delivered with **500 Global** as the **Georgia Startup Academy**, and reported **stage grants of 50,000 GEL and 150,000 GEL** have gone to startups advancing through it. (These programme figures come from agency and 500 Global announcements rather than the statute, and rounds, budgets and partners change — the long-running 500 Global accelerator partnership, for instance, was reported to conclude in mid-2026 — so confirm the current call before relying on any number.) On the broader business side, **Enterprise Georgia**, the agency under the Ministry of Economy, supports company creation, refurbishment and exports, mainly through the **"Produce in Georgia"** framework.

## Which regime fits — startup status vs the established options

Because the startup status **cannot be combined** with the Virtual Zone or International Company regimes, the choice matters. As a rough guide:

- An **early-stage, investment- or GITA-grant-funded** innovative company that is hiring in Georgia gains most from the **Innovative Startup Status**, where the first three years of salary are income-tax-free.
- An **established IT company exporting software** is usually better served by the [Virtual Zone Person](https://relocation.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-georgia/) regime (0% profit tax on foreign-sourced IT income) or, for a larger operation meeting the substance tests, the [International Company status](https://relocation.ge/en/international-company-status-georgia/).
- A **solo founder or freelancer** testing an idea may simply register as an [Individual Entrepreneur with Small Business Status](https://relocation.ge/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely/) and pay **1%** on turnover until the business grows.

Whichever route you pick, the practical groundwork is the same: [registering the company](https://relocation.ge/en/registering-a-company-in-georgia/), opening [business banking](https://relocation.ge/en/banking-in-georgia/), and — if you or your team will work from Georgia on local activity — checking the [right to work and work residence permit](https://relocation.ge/en/work-residence-permit-georgia/) rules introduced in the 2026 labour-migration reform.

## A note on a new framework

This regime is **less than a year old** and still bedding in. The statuses came into force in late September 2025, the implementing government resolution sets procedural detail that may be refined, and the grant and acceleration programmes around them run in time-limited rounds. The **English** consolidated text on the Legislative Herald lags the Georgian, so rely on the **Georgian** law and the official **innovationstatus.gov.ge** portal for the current rules. Confirm eligibility, thresholds, fees and open calls with **GITA**, the **Ministry of Economy and Sustainable Development** and **Enterprise Georgia**, and take professional tax advice before choosing a status — especially given the hard bar on combining the startup status with the Virtual Zone or International Company regimes.
