Georgia's public holidays are set by **Article 30 of the Labour Code**. There are **fourteen fixed dates** plus an **Easter block of four consecutive days**, which moves with the Orthodox calendar — **eighteen days in a typical year**.

Why "typical": because Easter moves, it can land on a fixed holiday. In **2029**, Easter Monday falls on **9 April**, the Day of National Unity — two holidays, one day off, so that year has **seventeen** days rather than eighteen. A calendar that prints a hardcoded 18 is wrong roughly once a decade.

Want the dates rather than the law? The **[Georgian Holiday Calendar](/en/georgia-holiday-calendar)** lists every year with a subscribable `.ics` feed and a JSON API, and computes the moving Easter dates for you.

Two things surprise newcomers. First, **Easter is Orthodox**, so it usually falls later than in Western Europe and lands on a different date each year. Second, the calendar is dense in the first half of the year: between January and May there are twelve holidays, and then a four-month gap until late August.

| **23 November** | St George's Day — *Giorgoba* |

## The Easter block — the one that moves

Article 30(1)(g) treats Easter as a **single block of four consecutive days**: **Good Friday, Holy Saturday, Easter Sunday** (Resurrection of Our Lord Jesus Christ) and **Easter Monday** (All Souls' Day). It is a **movable feast** — the dates change every year with the Orthodox calendar.

| Year | Easter Sunday | The four-day block |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2026** | Sunday **12 April** | **10–13 April** |
| **2027** | Sunday **2 May** | **30 April – 3 May** |

Because the block reaches from Friday to Monday, it produces the longest continuous shutdown of the year outside New Year. If you are planning travel, a property purchase, a bank appointment or anything at the Public Service Hall in that window, move it.

## What the law actually gives you

Article 30 is short, and two of its three paragraphs are about **flexibility** rather than the list itself. They are worth reading properly, because they cut in opposite directions.

**You may ask to swap a holiday for a different rest day.** Under **Article 30(2)**, an employee has the **right to request other rest days** in place of the statutory holidays, and those replacement days are then **determined by the employment agreement**. Note the shape of this: it is a right to *request* and to have the outcome written into the contract — not a right to take a different day unilaterally. If you would rather work on 9 May and take a day in August, that is a conversation with your employer, and the answer belongs in your agreement.

**The Government can add days off — but those are weaker.** Under **Article 30(3)**, the Government may declare **further days off by ordinance**, which in practice is how a holiday falling next to a weekend gets bridged. The catch is in the same paragraph: where a day off comes from a **government ordinance** rather than from the Labour Code itself, the **employer may require the employee to work instead on their next rest day** (Article 24(7)). So a bonus day announced by ordinance is not quite the same thing as a holiday listed in Article 30 — it can be moved onto you.

## What closes

The statute governs employment; it does not tell you which shop will be shut. In practice:

**Closed on the major holidays** — banks, the **[Public Service Hall](/en/personal-number-and-tax-id-georgia)** and government offices, most companies and offices. Public transport typically runs a reduced timetable.

**Usually open** — shops, cafés, restaurants and pharmacies, though often on shorter hours. Tourist areas keep working, particularly around Easter and the summer holidays.

**Longest shutdowns** — the **New Year period** (1–2 January, with many businesses effectively closed until after 7 January) and the **Easter block**. Anything administrative — a bank account, a residence-permit appointment, a company filing — should be scheduled clear of both.

## For employers

If you employ staff in Georgia, the holiday list is a floor, not a ceiling: your **employment agreements** can provide more, and Article 30(2) means an employee can ask to shift a statutory holiday to another rest day, with the result recorded in the contract. The broader framework — contracts, working time, leave, notice and payroll — is in **[Employing Staff in Georgia](/en/employing-staff-in-georgia)**.

## A note on scope

This page reproduces **Article 30** and explains what it says. It does **not** cover how work performed on a holiday is paid, or how holidays interact with annual leave and overtime — those sit in other articles of the Labour Code and in your contract. The English consolidated text on the Legislative Herald can lag the Georgian, and the Government may add days off during the year, so **confirm the current position before relying on a specific date**, particularly for anything with a deadline. This is information about the law as published — it is **not legal advice**.
