**VAT** (value-added tax) is Georgia's main tax on transactions. The **standard rate is 18%**, it applies to most supplies of goods and services made in Georgia and to imports, and a business must **register once its taxable turnover exceeds GEL 100,000** in any continuous 12-month period. This guide sets out the rate, the registration threshold, the categories of supply, the **reverse charge** on services from abroad, the rules for **digital services**, and filing — checked against the **Tax Code of Georgia** and the **Revenue Service**.

## The standard rate: 18%

Georgia's **standard VAT rate is 18%**. It applies to the **supply of goods and services for consideration** carried out within Georgian territory in the course of economic activity, and to **taxable imports**. VAT generally becomes chargeable at the moment of supply, and also applies to **advances** received for goods or services to be supplied later. The taxable amount is based on the consideration received or receivable. Because rates can change, **confirm the current rate with the Revenue Service** before relying on it.

## Registration: the GEL 100,000 threshold

| Situation | VAT registration |
| --- | --- |
| Taxable turnover exceeds **GEL 100,000** in any continuous **12-month** period | **Mandatory** — must apply to register |
| Georgian fixed establishment of a foreign taxable person | **Mandatory** from the start of taxable operations |
| Taxable turnover below the threshold | **Voluntary** registration available |

Registration is **mandatory** once a person's **taxable turnover exceeds GEL 100,000** measured over **any continuous 12 calendar months** — it is not tied to the calendar year, so the count rolls forward continuously. A person must apply to the tax authority promptly after crossing the threshold. A business **below** the threshold can choose to **register voluntarily**, which can be worthwhile where it wants to recover input VAT or supply VAT-registered customers.

## Categories of supply: taxable, exempt, zero-rated

Not every supply carries 18% VAT. Supplies fall into three broad categories:

| Category | VAT treatment | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard-rated** | 18% | Most goods and services supplied in Georgia |
| **Zero-rated** | 0% (taxable, with input-VAT recovery) | Exports and certain international supplies |
| **Exempt** | No VAT charged | Many financial services; certain medical and education services; goods and services for oil and gas operations |

The practical difference between **zero-rated** and **exempt** is input VAT: a zero-rated supplier generally **recovers** the VAT on its own costs, whereas an exempt supplier usually **cannot**. The precise scope of each exemption and zero-rating is set out in the Tax Code and should be checked supply by supply.

## Reverse charge: services from non-residents

When a **Georgian VAT payer buys services from a non-resident** who is not a registered VAT payer in Georgia, the tax is accounted for under the **reverse-charge mechanism**: the **Georgian recipient** declares and accounts for the VAT itself, as if it had made the supply, rather than the foreign supplier charging Georgian VAT. This is a routine obligation for Georgian businesses that buy consultancy, software, marketing and similar services from abroad, and it should be built into VAT reporting.

## VAT on digital services

Since **2021**, **non-residents with no establishment in Georgia** that supply **digital services to individuals** in Georgia must **register, declare and pay VAT** in Georgia. A service is treated as supplied in Georgia based on connecting factors such as the customer's location, the IP address of the device used, the country phone code, or the location of the payment institution. Under this regime **reporting is quarterly**, and payment may be made in **GEL, USD or EUR**. Whether a particular service falls within these rules depends on the details, so confirm the position with the Revenue Service.

## Filing and payment

The VAT **reporting period is the calendar month**. Registered VAT payers file a **monthly VAT return** and pay the tax, generally by the **15th day of the following month**, **electronically** through the Revenue Service portal at **[rs.ge](https://rs.ge/)**. The separate regime for **non-resident digital-service providers reports quarterly**. Deadlines and the exact filing obligations depend on your circumstances, so confirm them with the Revenue Service or an adviser.

## How VAT fits with the rest of the system

VAT is a **transaction tax** and is **separate from profit tax**: a company can owe VAT regardless of whether it is distributing profit, and the two are reported and paid separately.

- **[Corporate (profit) tax](/en/corporate-profit-tax-georgia)** — the distribution-based "Estonian" model that taxes company profit when it is distributed.
- **[Special tax regimes](/en/special-tax-regimes-georgia)** — the small, micro and fixed-status regimes for individual entrepreneurs and very small businesses. Note that being in a special regime does **not** automatically remove VAT obligations: the GEL 100,000 turnover threshold can still trigger mandatory VAT registration.

This guide is general information, **not tax advice**. The scope of exemptions and zero-rating, the place-of-supply rules and the reverse-charge and digital-services regimes are detailed and fact-specific, and the figures can change, so take professional advice and confirm the current rules with the **Revenue Service** before relying on a particular treatment.
