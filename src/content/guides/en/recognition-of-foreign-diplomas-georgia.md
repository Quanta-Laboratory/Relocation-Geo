If you are bringing a qualification into Georgia, or planning to use a Georgian degree elsewhere, the key institution is the **National Center for Educational Quality Enhancement (NCEQE)**. It is the official body for both directions of recognition, and understanding what it does — and does not — decide will save a lot of confusion.

## What the NCEQE does

The NCEQE recognises education acquired abroad: it verifies that a foreign educational document is authentic and establishes how the qualification, or the learning outcomes achieved, compare with the qualifications that exist in Georgia. It is also the body that provides verification, legalisation and apostille certification of educational documents issued in Georgia.

## Bringing a foreign qualification to Georgia

The most common reason international students need recognition is enrolment: a foreign school-leaving certificate (for a bachelor's) or a prior degree (for a master's) usually has to be recognised before you can study. Recognition can take time, so begin it early — it is frequently a precondition for admission, as covered in the [admissions and enrolment guide](https://relocation.ge/en/admissions-and-enrolment-georgia/).

## Using a Georgian diploma abroad

Graduating from a Georgian university is only half the story if you intend to work or study in another country. The NCEQE legalises or apostilles your Georgian educational documents so they are recognised internationally. But final acceptance always rests with the destination country's education authorities or professional bodies — Georgia can certify the document; it cannot bind another state to accept the qualification.

## Academic recognition vs professional licensing

A crucial distinction: academic recognition is not the same as the right to practise. Regulated professions — medicine above all — are governed by separate licensing authorities and examinations in each country. A recognised academic degree is usually a prerequisite, not a substitute, for licensing. Anyone studying toward a regulated profession should check the licensing rules of the country where they intend to work, as explained for doctors in the [studying medicine guide](https://relocation.ge/en/studying-medicine-in-georgia/).

## Practical tips

Prepare original documents plus certified translations, confirm whether your country needs an apostille or consular legalisation, and keep copies of every certified document. Where recognition is required for admission, treat it as the first task in your timeline rather than the last.
