The **immigration visa (category D)** is Georgia's **long-term** visa category. It is intended for foreign nationals who plan to relocate to Georgia for purposes such as work, business, study, family reunification or qualifying property ownership. 

In many cases, a **D visa** serves as the basis for applying for a **Georgian residence permit**, although Georgian law also allows certain foreign nationals who are already **lawfully present in Georgia** to apply for a residence permit directly from within the country. Choose the D subcategory that matches the purpose of your stay.

## The subcategories

- **D1 — Work and business.** For a foreign national who has obtained the **right to labour activity**, and for those coming to Georgia to carry out **entrepreneurial activity** in accordance with the **Law of Georgia on Entrepreneurs**.
- **D2 — Missions, freelancers and media.** For persons participating in scientific, sporting, cultural or educational missions; freelancers; interns and volunteers; and representatives of the mass media performing official duties.
- **D3 — Study and research.** For **adult** foreign nationals coming to Georgia to study or conduct research at an authorised educational institution, or to study under recognised international educational programmes.
- **D4 — Family reunification.** For foreign nationals arriving in Georgia for the purpose of family reunification.
- **D5 — Property owners.** For owners of Georgian real property (other than agricultural land) with a market value exceeding the GEL equivalent of **USD 150,000**, together with their spouse and child(ren). The market value must be confirmed by a **certified property valuer accredited by the Georgian Accreditation Centre**.
- **D6 — Minor students (from 1 September 2026).** For a **minor** studying at an authorised higher education institution, vocational education institution or general education institution in Georgia. Where the student is a minor, the category also covers the student's **parents or other legal representatives** and **minor siblings** until the student reaches **18 years of age**. The D6 category takes effect on **1 September 2026**. From the same date, **D3** is limited to **adult** students and researchers. See the **[D6 Visa Guide](/en/d6-visa-georgia)**.
    

## Type and validity

D visas are **multiple-entry immigration visas**.

Depending on the applicable category, they may be issued with a validity period of **90 calendar days** or **one year**. From **1 September 2026**, a **one-year D visa** may be issued only under the **D5** and **D6** categories. The remaining D categories are issued with a validity period of **90 calendar days**. Until that date, the one-year validity applies only to **D5**.

A D visa is intended for foreign nationals relocating to Georgia for long-term purposes and may, where applicable, serve as the basis for applying for a **Georgian residence permit**.

## Processing time

Applications for **D-category immigration visas** are processed by the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Georgia** through Georgian diplomatic missions and consular offices.

The statutory processing time is:

- **up to 30 calendar days** for all D-category visas, except **D5**; and
- **up to 10 calendar days** for **D5** visas.

Foreign nationals who are already **lawfully present in Georgia** and are eligible to apply for a D visa from within the country should submit their application **no later than 45 calendar days** before their authorised period of stay expires.

## Extensions

The **D3** (study and research) and **D5** (property ownership) immigration visas may be **extended**, provided the statutory conditions continue to be satisfied.

For **D5** extensions, applicants are **not required** to submit a new property valuation report.

Applications to extend **D3** and **D5** visas are submitted to the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)**, including through **Public Service Halls**.

## The 2026 right-to-work reform 

Since **1 March 2026**, foreign nationals carrying out **labour activity** in Georgia generally follow a two-stage process.

First, they obtain the required **Right to Labour Activity**. Once that right has been granted, they may apply for the appropriate **D1 immigration visa** or, where permitted, a **Work Residence Permit**.

Holding a **temporary residence permit** does **not**, by itself, confer the right to carry out labour activity in Georgia.

For further information, see the **[Right to Work Guide](/en/right-to-work-georgia)** and the **[Work Residence Permit Guide](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia)**.

## From a D visa to a residence permit

A D-category immigration visa allows eligible foreign nationals to enter Georgia for a long-term purpose.

If you intend to remain in Georgia beyond the period authorised by your visa, you may apply for the **appropriate residence permit**, provided you satisfy the statutory requirements. Depending on the purpose of your stay, this may include, for example:

- **D1** → **[Work Residence Permit](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia)**
- **D5** → **[Short-Term Residence Permit by Property](/en/short-term-residence-permit-property-georgia)**

In many cases, a D visa serves as the basis for applying for a residence permit. However, Georgian law also allows certain foreign nationals who are **already lawfully present in Georgia**—including many persons benefiting from Georgia's visa-free regime—to apply for a residence permit directly from within Georgia, provided they meet the applicable legal requirements.

For a complete overview of the available residence-permit categories, see the **[Guide to Residence Permits](/en/guide-to-residence-permits)**.
