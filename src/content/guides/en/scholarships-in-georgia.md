Scholarships are available for international students in Georgia, although they are generally more limited than some promotional materials may suggest. In practice, funding opportunities usually fall into three categories: **European Union programmes**, **government or bilateral scholarship schemes**, and **university-specific scholarships or tuition discounts**. Because tuition fees and living costs are comparatively affordable, many international students choose to finance their studies independently.

## Erasmus+ and European Union programmes

The most significant international funding opportunities are provided through the **European Union's Erasmus+ programme**. Many Georgian universities participate in Erasmus+ mobility, enabling students to study, train or undertake exchanges between Georgian and European higher education institutions.

Students may also be eligible to apply for **Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's scholarships**, which fund selected international master's programmes delivered by consortia of universities in different countries. These scholarships are highly competitive but are among the most comprehensive sources of funding available for graduate study.

## Georgian government and bilateral scholarships

Most Georgian government financial support is intended for domestic students. Nevertheless, some scholarship opportunities are available to international students through **bilateral agreements**, cultural exchange programmes or other targeted government initiatives.

The availability of these schemes varies from year to year. Prospective students should therefore consult the **Ministry of Education, Science and Youth of Georgia**, as well as the relevant embassy or government authority in their home country, for current opportunities.

## University scholarships and tuition discounts

Many Georgian universities offer their own **merit-based scholarships**, **tuition discounts** or other financial incentives for high-performing international applicants.

These schemes are administered independently by each university, so applicants should contact the admissions office directly for information about eligibility, application procedures and whether any award applies for a single academic year or for the full duration of the programme.

## Self-funding remains the most common option

Because scholarship opportunities are relatively limited, most international students finance their studies themselves. Fortunately, Georgia's comparatively low tuition fees and affordable cost of living make this a realistic option for many students.

Our [Tuition and Cost of Studying in Georgia](https://relocation.ge/en/tuition-and-cost-of-studying-georgia/) guide provides indicative figures to help you plan your budget.

## How to approach funding

If you intend to apply for financial support, begin your search as early as possible.

Check **Erasmus+** opportunities through both your home and prospective host university, enquire directly with Georgian universities about institutional scholarships or tuition discounts, and investigate any bilateral scholarship programmes available between Georgia and your home country.

It is sensible to treat scholarships as an opportunity to reduce your overall costs, rather than relying on them as the sole means of financing your studies.
