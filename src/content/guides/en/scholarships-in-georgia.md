Scholarships exist for international students in Georgia, but they are more limited than the country's marketing sometimes suggests. The realistic picture is a mix of EU programmes, a small set of government and bilateral schemes, and university-level discounts — set against tuition and living costs that are already low enough that many students self-fund.

## Erasmus+ and EU programmes

The most significant external funding route is the EU's **Erasmus+** programme. Georgian universities participate in Erasmus+ mobility, and students can also access **Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's** scholarships, which fund study across several universities — typically combining Georgian and European institutions — and can cover study, research and traineeship periods. These are competitive but among the most valuable awards available.

## Georgian government and bilateral scholarships

The Georgian state's funding is directed mainly at domestic students. For international students there are some **bilateral and targeted scholarships**, often arranged between Georgia and specific partner countries or through cultural and exchange agreements. Availability changes year to year, so the Ministry of Education and Science is the place to check what is currently open.

## University scholarships and discounts

Individual universities sometimes offer their own **merit-based tuition discounts or partial scholarships**, particularly to attract strong international applicants. These are decided by each institution rather than centrally, so the admissions office is the right contact. When comparing offers, look at whether a discount applies for one year or the whole programme.

## Self-funding is the common path

Because scholarship places are limited, most international students fund their own studies — and Georgia makes that feasible. Tuition for English-taught programmes and the cost of living are both low by international standards, so a full degree is achievable on a modest budget. The [tuition and cost-of-studying guide](https://relocation.ge/en/tuition-and-cost-of-studying-georgia/) sets out typical figures to plan around.

## How to approach funding

Apply early and in parallel: check Erasmus+ opportunities through your home and host universities, ask each Georgian university directly about institutional scholarships, and look at any bilateral schemes between Georgia and your country. Treat scholarships as a bonus to a plan that already works financially, rather than the foundation of it.
