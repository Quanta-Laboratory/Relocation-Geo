This guide covers **importing a car into Georgia** — clearing it through customs and registering it — and **buying a car that is already in Georgia**. For driving licences, foreign plates and insurance, see the [driving in Georgia](/en/driving-in-georgia) guide. This is general information; confirm current rules and figures before importing.

Two authorities are involved:

- the **Revenue Service** handles **customs clearance** and import taxes;
- the **Service Agency of the Ministry of Internal Affairs** handles **registration and plates**.

## Step 1 — Customs clearance and import taxes

When you bring a vehicle into Georgia to keep it here, it must be **cleared through customs** with the Revenue Service. Up to three charges can apply:

- **Import (customs) duty** — a duty on the vehicle's customs value;
- **Excise** — based on **engine capacity and vehicle age** (see below);
- **VAT** — Georgia's value added tax on imported goods.

How these combine, and the exact duty and VAT applicable to your car, depend on the vehicle and its customs valuation. **Confirm the current import duty and VAT with the Revenue Service** before clearing the car, and use the Revenue Service's customs valuation for your specific vehicle rather than an unofficial estimate.

### How excise works

Excise on passenger cars is charged **per cubic centimetre (cm³) of engine capacity**, with the rate depending on the car's **age**. Under amendments to the Tax Code approved by the Parliament of Georgia in 2026:

| Vehicle age | Excise rate (basic) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 6 years inclusive | GEL 1.5 per cm³ |
| Older than 6 years | GEL 4.5 per cm³ |

Special rates apply to some vehicles:

- **Left-hand-drive hybrid** vehicles: a preferential rate **reduced by 60%** of the basic rate;
- **Right-hand-drive** vehicles: **three times** the basic rate.

For imports, a vehicle's **age** is generally measured as the difference between the year the customs declaration is registered and the year of manufacture. **Electric vehicles** have no engine displacement, so the displacement-based excise does not apply in the same way — confirm their treatment with the Revenue Service.

The 2026 amendments also changed the **classic-car** age threshold: a vehicle is now treated as a classic if it is **more than 30 years old** (previously 40). Transition rules applied to vehicles imported, or whose transport to Georgia began, before **1 April 2026**. Because excise rules and transition dates change, **confirm the current rate and any transition provisions with the Revenue Service**.

## Step 2 — Register the car and get Georgian plates

After customs clearance, register the vehicle at the **Service Agency of the Ministry of Internal Affairs**. Registration is done at the Agency's registration service regardless of your home or legal address.

**Deadlines.** A vehicle brought in **for the purpose of registration** must be registered **within 60 days**. A vehicle kept in the country for **more than 90 days** is subject to (temporary) registration at the Service Agency. Confirm the current deadlines with the Service Agency and Revenue Service.

**Documents for first registration:**

- an **application** (completed at the office);
- an **identity document**;
- a document confirming **ownership** of the vehicle;
- an **inspection (dathvaliereba) act**;
- receipts for the **statutory fees**;
- **internal transit**, where applicable.

**Registration fees:**

| Item | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle inspection (dathvaliereba) act | GEL 20 |
| State number plate | GEL 80 |
| Vehicle registration certificate | GEL 100 |

On registration, the Service Agency records the vehicle's details — make, model, body type, **VIN**, year of manufacture, colour, **engine number and capacity**, fuel type, permitted and unladen weight, and any legal encumbrance — and issues the **state plate** and **registration certificate**.

## Buying a car already in Georgia

If you buy a car that is **already registered in Georgia**, you do not import it. Instead you complete a **re-registration (transfer of ownership)** at the Service Agency when the owner changes through sale, gift or exchange.

**Documents for a transfer:**

- the document confirming **ownership** of the vehicle;
- the **identity document(s)**;
- an **inspection act**;
- a **joint application** (buyer and seller);
- receipts for the statutory fees.

**Transfer fees** are the same as for registration:

| Item | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle inspection act | GEL 20 |
| State number plate | GEL 80 |
| Vehicle registration certificate | GEL 100 |

After the transfer, the vehicle is registered in the buyer's name and a new registration certificate is issued.

## Before you import or buy

- Get the **customs valuation and tax figures** for your specific vehicle from the Revenue Service before clearing it.
- Remember the **excise depends on engine capacity and age**, with special rates for hybrid and right-hand-drive vehicles.
- Budget for the **registration fees** (GEL 20 + GEL 80 + GEL 100) at the Service Agency, on top of any import taxes.
- For licences, foreign plates and compulsory insurance, see [driving in Georgia](/en/driving-in-georgia).

This is general information; confirm the current requirement, duty or fee with the **Revenue Service** and the **Service Agency** before importing or registering.
