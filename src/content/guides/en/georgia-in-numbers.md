Georgia is a small, open, upper-middle-income economy at the crossroads of Europe and Asia, on the eastern shore of the Black Sea. With a population of about **3.9 million** and a land area of roughly **69,700 km²**, it combines a modest domestic market with an outward-facing economy built on trade, transit, tourism and a fast-growing services sector. Over the past decade it has been one of the faster-growing economies in the wider region, supported by low taxes, a liberal business environment and deepening ties with both the European Union and its neighbours. This page pulls together the headline figures from official sources — chiefly the National Statistics Office of Georgia (Geostat) and the National Bank of Georgia — and sets them against a regional and global backdrop.

## People and population

Georgia's 2024 population census recorded **3,929,581 residents** as of 14 November 2024 — up from the 2014 count, largely on the back of an urban rebound. About **62% of the population now lives in urban areas**, and the capital, **Tbilisi, is home to roughly 1.33 million people, or around 34% of the country** (Geostat, 2024 census). The population is concentrated heavily in and around Tbilisi and the Black Sea corridor, while many rural and mountainous areas are sparsely settled. For a detailed breakdown of who is moving in and out of Georgia — and how the 2022 regional inflows reshaped the numbers — see [Migration to Georgia: what the official data shows](/en/migration-to-georgia-data).

## The economy

Georgia's output is dominated by **services**, with **industry** second and **agriculture** now a relatively small share of value added — a structure typical of a middle-income economy shifting toward trade, tourism and business services.

Shares are of gross value added (Geostat, 2024); the remainder is net taxes on products. Within services, the largest single contributor to nominal GDP is wholesale and retail trade, followed by real estate, with manufacturing and construction leading on the industry side.

Key sectors underpinning the economy include:

- **Tourism** — a major source of foreign-currency earnings and a large employer, driving much of the services surplus.
- **IT / BPO and ICT** — a fast-growing export-oriented segment, supported by special tax regimes for the sector.
- **Agriculture and wine** — smaller in GDP terms but culturally central and a notable export earner, with wine a flagship product.
- **Transit and logistics** — Georgia's position on east–west corridors between the Caspian, the Black Sea and Türkiye makes trans-shipment and re-export significant.
- **Hydropower and energy** — abundant hydro resources support domestic supply and periodic electricity exports.
- **Construction and real estate** — consistently among the largest contributors to nominal GDP.
- **Financial services** — a well-developed banking sector, reflected in it being the single largest recipient of foreign direct investment.

## Trade: exports and imports

Georgia runs a large **merchandise trade deficit**. In 2024, total goods trade turnover was about **USD 23.4 billion**, with exports of roughly **USD 6.6 billion** against imports of about **USD 16.9 billion**, leaving a goods trade deficit of around **USD 10.3 billion** (Geostat, 2024). Much of this gap is offset by a strong surplus in **services — above all tourism** — which does not appear in the merchandise figures.

Georgia's export basket is concentrated, and heavily shaped by **re-exports** (notably used cars re-shipped to neighbouring markets):

Businesses trading in or through Georgia often make use of its [free industrial zones](/en/free-industrial-zones-georgia), which offer customs and tax advantages for manufacturing and re-export.

## Business climate

Georgia consistently ranks as one of the more open and lightly regulated economies in the region. It scored around **69 and ranked roughly 35th globally in the Heritage Foundation's 2025 Index of Economic Freedom** — the highest in the South Caucasus, ahead of Armenia, Azerbaijan and Türkiye. On governance, it scored **53 out of 100 in Transparency International's 2024 Corruption Perceptions Index**, placing it in the middle of the global table, though its score has slipped in the most recent readings.

**Foreign direct investment** has cooled from earlier peaks: net FDI was about **USD 1.57 billion in 2024** (adjusted data, Geostat), down from prior years, with the bulk being reinvested earnings. The largest investor economies were the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and Malta, and the financial sector was the single biggest recipient.

A large part of Georgia's appeal to entrepreneurs and relocating businesses is its **low, simple tax system**. Companies benefit from an Estonian-style [corporate profit tax](/en/corporate-profit-tax-georgia) that is only charged on distributed profit, alongside [special tax regimes](/en/special-tax-regimes-georgia) for small businesses, IT and international companies. Individuals may also qualify for favourable [tax residency](/en/tax-residency-georgia) treatment.

## Why this matters for relocation

For anyone considering a move, the numbers tell a consistent story: a **small but fast-growing, open economy**, with **low inflation historically**, a **liberal tax system**, and the **highest economic-freedom ranking in the South Caucasus**. Services and trade dominate, the population is concentrated in Tbilisi, and the country's position on regional trade corridors keeps it outward-looking. If you are weighing Georgia as a base, these fundamentals sit behind the practical questions covered elsewhere on this site — from the [work and residence permit](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia) route to [tax residency](/en/tax-residency-georgia) and the [migration data](/en/migration-to-georgia-data) that shows who else is making the move.
