Setting up a company in Georgia is governed by the Law of Georgia on Entrepreneurs (No 875 of 2021, in force since 1 January 2022). The law is open to foreigners: foreign individuals and foreign-registered legal entities can found and own a Georgian company on the same terms as locals, and there is no residency requirement to be a partner or a director.

## Business forms

Business activity can be carried out either as an [individual entrepreneur](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely) or as a company. The law recognises five company forms: the general partnership, the limited partnership, the limited liability company (LLC), the joint-stock company (JSC) and the cooperative. A company's brand name must indicate its form — for example "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company". For most foreigners setting up a small or medium business, the LLC is the standard choice.

## Liability: the key difference between forms

The forms differ mainly in how partners answer for the company's debts. Partners of an LLC, a JSC and a cooperative, and the limited partners of a limited partnership, are not liable to creditors for the company's obligations — their risk is limited to their investment. By contrast, the partners of a general partnership, and the general partners of a limited partnership, are jointly, severally and personally liable with all their assets. This is why the LLC, with its limited liability, is so widely used.

## Capital

For an LLC, the law does not prescribe a minimum capital amount. The capital is divided into shares, and the partners' shareholdings are expressed in percentages that together equal 100%. A company may also set an authorised capital — a ceiling for issuing further shares in future — but it is not obligatory.

## How registration works

Registration of a company is mandatory and is carried out by the registration authority, the National Agency of Public Registry. Crucially, registration entails both state registration and tax registration in a single step: the registration authority sends the data electronically to the Revenue Service, so there is no separate tax-registration procedure. A company is deemed established from the moment of registration, and the registration decision takes effect once it is published on the authority's central electronic platform.

## What you need to register

To register a company you submit its instrument of incorporation (the charter or partners' agreement), which must set out the brand name, the legal address, the identification details of each partner or founder, and the person with management and representation powers (and a supervisory board, if there is one). The legal address must be the company's actual address in Georgia. Once registered, the company's data is public — anyone can view the registry and obtain an extract, and the documents filed at registration are published online free of charge.

## Foreign companies and redomiciliation

A company registered abroad can operate in Georgia through a branch, which is registered with the same authority. The law also allows an entrepreneur registered in another country to redomicile its registration to Georgia, moving the company itself rather than setting up a new one.

Once the company exists, see [running a company in Georgia](/en/running-a-company-in-georgia) for the governance and ongoing obligations that follow.
