The ordinary visa — category **C** — is the everyday short-term visa for travellers who need a visa to enter Georgia. It is one of the five visa categories in Article 7 of the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons. Before reading on: if your nationality is on the [visa-free list](/en/visa-free-entry-georgia), you generally do **not** need a C visa at all — you can enter and stay up to a year without one. Category C matters mainly for nationalities that require a visa.

## The five subcategories

- **C1 — tourism.** For individuals arriving for tourism purposes.
- **C2 — relatives and friends.** For individuals visiting relatives and friends in Georgia.
- **C3 — business and professional.** Business meetings and negotiations; participants in scientific workshops, conferences and other research, pedagogical, cultural or sporting events; journalists on assignment; crew members of ships in Georgian harbours (unless a treaty provides visa-free crew movement); principal drivers and co-drivers on international cargo and passenger transport.
- **C4 — humanitarian and personal.** Receiving or providing humanitarian assistance in life- or health-threatening situations; charity work; guardians or wards of a Georgian citizen; medical treatment; visiting graves of relatives and friends; pilgrimage and religious contacts.
- **C5 — work for a non-resident (remote work).** For short-term visitors who, while in Georgia, carry out activities **only in favour of a non-resident person**, where those activities relate to the non-resident's activity outside Georgia. This is the closest fit to a "remote worker" visa. It can be issued with five-year validity and a one-year stay, and also covers the holder's spouse and minor children.

## Type and validity

The ordinary visa is a **short-term** visa. A single-entry short-term visa is issued for up to 30 days. Multiple-entry short-term visas can carry validity of up to five years. As noted, C5 may be issued for five-year validity with a one-year right of stay.

## How to apply

Eligible nationals can apply online through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs **e-visa** portal and receive an electronic visa without visiting a consulate; the same portal handles consular applications, current eligibility and fees.

## Working on a C visa — the 2026 change

C5 is designed around work for a non-resident, but for paid work performed **inside** Georgia the rules changed on **1 March 2026**: most foreign nationals must obtain a separate "Right to Labour Activity" permit before doing paid employment, self-employment or entrepreneurial activity. Holding a visa is not the same as holding the right to work — see the [Right to Work guide](/en/right-to-work-georgia) if you plan to earn income here.

## Planning a longer stay?

The C visa is for short visits. To live in Georgia on a stable, renewable footing you move to an [immigration (D) visa](/en/immigration-visa-georgia), which is the precondition for a [residence permit](/en/guide-to-residence-permits).
