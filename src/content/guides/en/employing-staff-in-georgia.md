Employing people in Georgia brings together three bodies of law: the **Organic Law – Labour Code of Georgia** for the employment relationship itself, the **Tax Code** for payroll withholding, and the **Law on Funded Pension** for the mandatory pension scheme. This guide sets out the essentials an employer needs — how the contract works, what comes off the payroll, and what must be reported to the **Revenue Service** — checked against the primary Georgian sources. It is general information, **not legal or tax advice**.

If you are still deciding on a structure, see [Run a Business in Georgia](/en/run-a-business-in-georgia) and [Running a Company in Georgia](/en/running-a-company-in-georgia); once you have a company, the rules below apply when you take on staff.

## The employment contract under the Labour Code

Georgian labour law is codified in the **Organic Law – Labour Code of Georgia**. A few basics shape almost every hire.

**Form of the agreement.** A labour agreement may be made **orally or in writing**, for a **definite or an indefinite** period. It **must be in writing** where the labour relationship continues for **more than three months**. In practice most employers use a written contract from day one.

**Trial (probation) period.** The parties may agree a **trial period of no more than six months**, and only **once** with a given employee, to check whether the person is fit for the work. A trial-period agreement must be **in writing**, and the work done during it **must be paid**.

**Working time.** The employee's **workweek must not exceed 40 hours**. In companies with specific operating conditions that require a continuous mode of work of more than eight hours, the ceiling is **48 hours** a week; the Government defines those sectors.

**Leave.** An employee is entitled to **paid annual leave of at least 24 working days** per year, and may also take **unpaid leave of at least 15 calendar days** per year. Notice and carry-forward rules for leave are set out in the Code.

**Public holidays.** On top of annual leave, the Code sets **18 statutory holidays in a typical year** — fourteen fixed dates plus the four-day Orthodox Easter block. An employee may ask to swap a holiday for another rest day, with the substitution recorded in the employment agreement. See **[Public Holidays in Georgia](/en/public-holidays-in-georgia)**.

**Termination and notice.** The Code lists the permitted **grounds for termination** and protects the employee with notice, severance and a right to reasons. At a high level, where the **employer** ends the contract it must either:

- give **at least 30 calendar days' prior written notice** and pay **severance of at least one month's salary**; or
- give **at least three calendar days' prior written notice** and pay **severance of at least two months' salary**.

The employee can request **written reasons** for a dismissal and, in defined cases, **appeal to the court**. Because dismissals are fact-specific and procedural, take advice before ending a contract.

## Payroll taxes: personal income tax

Salaries are subject to **personal income tax**, which Georgia levies at a **flat 20%**. For employees the tax is collected by **withholding at source**: the employer, acting as a tax agent, deducts the tax from the salary and pays it to the Revenue Service, so many employees never file a return themselves. The rate and the withholding mechanism are set out in the **Tax Code**; the general 20% rate is covered in the [Personal Income Tax in Georgia](/en/personal-income-tax-georgia) guide.

## Payroll taxes: the mandatory funded pension

On top of income tax, most salaries are subject to the **mandatory funded pension scheme**, administered by the **Pension Agency** since 1 January 2019. The **National Bank of Georgia** describes it as built on the **2% + 2% + 2% principle** of accumulation:

| Contribution | Who pays it | Rate of the untaxed salary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee | Withheld by the employer from the employee's salary | 2% |
| Employer | Paid by the employer on top of salary | 2% |
| State | Paid by the state for the employee | up to 2% |

The **state** contribution is **up to 2%** of the untaxed salary and **tapers for higher earners** — it reduces above a defined salary level and stops above a higher one. Because the exact thresholds are periodically adjusted, **confirm the current figures with the Pension Agency**.

### Who is enrolled — and foreign staff

Enrolment in the mandatory scheme is generally required for **Georgian citizens**, for **foreign citizens who hold a Georgian permanent residence permit**, and for **stateless persons permanently resident** in Georgia. **Foreign citizens without permanent residence** in Georgia are generally **outside** the mandatory scheme, so contributions are typically not made for them.

Participation is, as a rule, **mandatory** for those in scope rather than optional, and the limited departure and exit rules sit in the **Law on Funded Pension**. Because the outcome depends on an individual worker's residence and citizenship status, **confirm each person's position with the Pension Agency** before setting up their payroll.

## Registering and reporting with the Revenue Service

An employer that pays salaries acts as a **tax agent** and must account to the **Revenue Service** for what it withholds. In practice this means:

- **registering** the business with the Revenue Service (company or Individual Entrepreneur registration also serves as tax registration) and operating through the **rs.ge** electronic portal;
- **withholding** the 20% personal income tax and the **2% employee pension** contribution from each salary, and adding the **2% employer** contribution;
- filing a **monthly payroll declaration** and **paying** the withheld tax and the pension contributions, **normally by the 15th of the month following** the salary payment.

Filing the declaration and transferring the money are **separate steps** — submitting the return does not itself move the funds. Deadlines, forms and procedures are set by the Tax Code and Revenue Service practice and can change, so **confirm the current rules with the Revenue Service** or an accountant.

## Hiring foreign staff: the right to work

Employing a **foreign national** adds an immigration layer on top of the contract and payroll duties. Under the **2026 labour-migration rules**, some foreigners must first obtain the **[right to work](/en/right-to-work-georgia)** before they can lawfully be employed in Georgia, although several categories are exempt. A foreign employee also typically needs the **[work residence permit](/en/work-residence-permit-georgia)** to live in Georgia on the basis of that employment.

The key point for an employer is that **you apply, not the candidate**. After the employment contract is concluded, the **local employer** files for the right to work through the Ministry's electronic labour-migration system, and the resulting permission is tied to **your company specifically** — it does not travel with the employee to another job. The Agency decides within a maximum of **30 calendar days** from a complete filing, or **10 working days** under the expedited procedure.

### The duties Ordinance No 321 added

**Government Ordinance No 321 of 9 July 2026**, in force the following day, put three further obligations on the employer.

**An annual quota, if you hire at scale.** If you plan to employ **more than five foreign nationals in a calendar year**, or foreign nationals would exceed **5% of your total headcount** (unless that 5% is fewer than five people), you must **agree an annual quota** with the Agency electronically — **at least ten working days before** you apply for the right to work. Applications then sit inside that quota, which can be raised on a reasoned request. A quota left substantially unused may be **cut the following year**, so do not over-ask.

**A turnover test, per foreign hire.** You must evidence, via the Revenue Service, **annual turnover of at least GEL 50,000 for each foreign national** you are applying for — **GEL 35,000** for educational and medical institutions. A **young company is excused**: no turnover certificate is needed where you have **no more than three foreign employees** and **no more than three months** have passed since founding or registration. If you are not VAT-registered, other competent certificates covering the previous twelve months serve instead.

**Registration of short-term professional activity.** If a foreign national comes in for **short-term professional activity** — an exempt category, but only if handled correctly — the **employer or the organiser of the activity** must register them in the labour-migration system at **labourmigration.moh.gov.ge** **before the activity starts**, submitting the prescribed details. The registration is **free**. Get it wrong — no registration, unconfirmed status, the person staying unlawfully, or an activity that falls under a quota — and it simply **is not** short-term professional activity: the work becomes unauthorised, and liability falls on the employer as well as the worker. Note also the four-month annual cap on this route.

Separately, the employer must record an **early termination of, or change to, the employment agreement** in the electronic system **within 5 calendar days**. Failing to report a termination, amendment or extension is itself fined.

Check both guides — and the **[Law on Labour Migration](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/2806732)** — before making an offer to a candidate who is not already entitled to work in Georgia. Ordinance No 321 has **no official English translation**; the summary above is our reading of the Georgian original.

## Getting it right

Employment, tax and pension rules are updated from time to time, and the figures above should be treated as a starting point rather than the last word. Give the **percentages and deadlines** a fresh check against the **Revenue Service** (income tax and reporting) and the **Pension Agency** (contributions and enrolment) before you run your first payroll, and take professional advice on contracts and dismissals. This guide is **general information, not legal or tax advice**.
