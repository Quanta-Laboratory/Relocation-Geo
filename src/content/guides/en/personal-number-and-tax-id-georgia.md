Two different identification numbers are commonly encountered when living or doing business in Georgia: the **personal number** shown on a Georgian residence card, and the **tax identification number (TIN)** issued by the Revenue Service. Which one you need depends primarily on whether you hold a **Georgian residence permit**.

## The personal number (residence card)

A Georgian **residence card** contains an **11-digit personal number**. For foreign nationals who have been granted a residence permit and entered into the Civil Registry, this number is also used as their **tax identification number** for tax purposes.

To obtain a residence card, you must first be granted a **Georgian residence permit**. The residence card is issued by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** through its territorial offices and **Public Service Halls**. For more information, see the **[Guide to Residence Permits](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-residence-permits/)**.

## The tax identification number (TIN)

Foreign nationals who **do not hold a Georgian residence permit** can still obtain a **9-digit tax identification number (TIN)** from the **Revenue Service of Georgia**. A residence permit is **not** required.

For many newcomers, remote workers and investors, a Revenue Service TIN is the first identification number obtained because it enables many everyday administrative and business activities.

There are two ways to obtain a TIN:

- **In person** at a Revenue Service service centre by presenting a valid foreign passport. The TIN is typically issued on the same day.
- **Online** through the Revenue Service's electronic services, using a video identity-verification procedure. At present, this verification must generally be completed while the applicant is **physically present in Georgia**.

## What are these numbers used for?

Depending on your circumstances, your **personal number** or **Revenue Service TIN** may be required when:

- opening a bank account (see the **[Banking Guide](https://relocation.ge/en/banking-in-georgia/)**);
- signing a lease;
- registering as an **Individual Entrepreneur** (see the **[Individual Entrepreneur Guide](https://relocation.ge/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely/)**);
- registering with the Revenue Service and filing tax returns; and
- using various government and administrative services.

## Which one do you need?

If you are opening a business bank account, registering an Individual Entrepreneur or carrying out other tax-related activities **before obtaining a residence permit**, a **Revenue Service TIN** will usually be sufficient.

Once you obtain a **Georgian residence permit** and are issued a **residence card**, your **11-digit personal number**becomes your principal identifier and is also used as your taxpayer identification number.

As administrative procedures may change, it is advisable to confirm the latest requirements with the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** and the **Revenue Service of Georgia** before applying.
