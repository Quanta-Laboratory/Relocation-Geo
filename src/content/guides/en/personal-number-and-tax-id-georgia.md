Two different numbers come up constantly when settling in Georgia: the **personal number** on a Georgian ID card, and a **tax identification number (TIN)**. Which one you need depends on whether you hold a residence permit, and knowing the difference saves a wasted trip to the wrong office.

## The personal number (ID card)

A Georgian ID card carries an **11-digit personal number**, and for residents this same number serves as the tax identification number across tax matters. To get it as a foreigner you first need a **residence permit**, which is the prerequisite for issuing the ID card. The card and number are issued by the **Public Service Development Agency (PSDA)** through Public Service Halls. Residence permits themselves are covered in the [guide to residence permits](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-residence-permits/).

## The tax identification number (TIN)

If you do not have a residence permit, you can still get a **9-digit tax identification number from the Revenue Service** — no residence permit required. This is what many newcomers, remote workers and investors obtain first, because it is enough for a great deal of practical business.

There are two ways to get it:

- **In person** at any Revenue Service office, with only a valid foreign passport — typically issued the same day.
- **Online** through the Revenue Service portal, with a video-call identity check that must be completed from inside Georgia (it cannot be done from abroad).

## What you use them for

Either number unlocks the practical steps of living here: opening a bank account (see the [banking guide](https://relocation.ge/en/banking-in-georgia/)), signing a lease, registering as an Individual Entrepreneur (the [IE guide](https://relocation.ge/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely/)), filing taxes, and dealing with government services. Banks and counterparties will routinely ask for your personal number or TIN.

## Which one do you need?

If you are simply opening a bank account or registering a small business and don't yet hold a residence permit, the **Revenue Service TIN** is usually the quickest path. If you are living here long-term on a residence permit, you will receive the **11-digit personal number** with your ID card, and that becomes your main identifier. Confirm current procedures with the PSDA and the Revenue Service, as administrative steps can change.
