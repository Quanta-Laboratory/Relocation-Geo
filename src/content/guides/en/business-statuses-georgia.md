faq:
  - q: Do I need a status to run a business in Georgia?
    a: No. A status is optional. Without one, an Individual Entrepreneur pays personal income tax and a company pays the standard 15% profit tax under the distribution-based (Estonian) model. A status can reduce or simplify that, but only if your activity and turnover qualify.
  - q: Which status is for a solo freelancer?
    a: Small Business Status, which is available to an Individual Entrepreneur and taxes turnover at 1% up to the statutory cap. It is the simplest and most common status for freelancers and small service providers.
  - q: Which status is for an IT company?
    a: "Two are aimed at IT: Virtual Zone Person status (0% profit tax on software supplied to customers outside Georgia) and International Company Status (5% profit tax and 5% salary tax for qualifying IT and maritime companies). The right choice depends on your size, staffing and exactly what you sell — the detailed guides compare them."
  - q: Can one company hold more than one status?
    a: "Generally no — the IT-focused statuses are alternatives, and each has its own conditions and exclusions. You choose the one that fits your activity. VASP registration is different: it is a regulatory authorisation to provide crypto services, not a tax status, and applies on top of your company form."
  - q: Is a status permanent?
    a: No. Statuses are granted on conditions and can be revoked if you stop meeting them (for example, exceeding a turnover cap, changing activity, or failing compliance requirements). Treat the linked guides as the current framework and confirm the position before you rely on it.

Registering a business gives you a legal form, such as an **Individual Entrepreneur** or a **company**. A **business status** is a separate, optional designation that may be available once the business has been registered. If you qualify, it changes how your business is taxed or regulated without changing its legal form.

The right status depends primarily on **the nature of your business activity** and, in some cases, **its size or turnover**.

## Business statuses

**[Small Business Status](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely)** — available only to **Individual Entrepreneurs** carrying on eligible activities. Qualifying businesses pay **1% tax on turnover** up to the statutory threshold, making it a popular choice for freelancers and other small businesses.

**[International Company Status](/en/international-company-status-georgia)** — designed for **companies** carrying on qualifying **IT or maritime activities**. It provides **reduced corporate and employment tax rates**, together with other tax incentives, and is intended for established businesses with genuine operations in Georgia.

**[Virtual Zone Person](/en/virtual-zone-person-georgia)** — available to **IT companies** that develop software or provide qualifying IT services to customers **outside Georgia**. Profits from qualifying foreign-source activities are **exempt from Georgian profit tax**, and the relevant supplies are generally **VAT-exempt**.

**[Startup & innovation statuses](/en/startup-support-georgia)** — available to qualifying **startups, innovative SMEs and R&D companies** under Georgia's innovation framework. Each status has its own eligibility criteria and tax incentives.

**[VASP registration](/en/vasp-georgia)** —  required for businesses providing **virtual asset (crypto) services**. It is a **regulatory authorisation**issued by the **National Bank of Georgia**, rather than a tax status, and operates alongside the company's legal form.

**[Investor guarantees](/en/investor-guarantees-in-georgia)** — not a business status, but a statutory investment protection regime. Foreign investors benefit from protections including **equal treatment**, **free repatriation of profits**, and **protection against unlawful expropriation**.
