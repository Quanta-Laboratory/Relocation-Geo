  - q: "Do I need a status to run a business in Georgia?"

Registering a business gives you a legal form (Individual Entrepreneur or company). A **status** is a separate, optional layer on top of that form: it changes how you are taxed or regulated, provided your activity qualifies. Choosing the right status is mostly about **what you do** and **how big you are**.

## The statuses, and who each is for

**[Small Business Status](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely)** — for an **Individual Entrepreneur** working solo. Taxes turnover at **1%** up to the statutory cap. The default choice for freelancers, consultants and small service providers.

**[International Company Status](/en/international-company-status-georgia)** — for **companies** in qualifying **IT and maritime** activities. Cuts profit tax to **5%** and salary income tax to **5%**, with property-tax relief. Aimed at established companies with real substance and staff in Georgia.

**[Virtual Zone Person](/en/virtual-zone-person-georgia)** — for **IT companies** that create software and supply it to customers **outside Georgia**. Profit from those foreign sales is **exempt from profit tax**, and the supply is VAT-exempt. Popular with software firms that export.

**[Startup & innovation statuses](/en/startup-support-georgia)** — for **early-stage and R&D companies** under the 2025 innovation reform (Innovative Startup, Innovative SME, R&D status), each with its own tax advantages.

**[VASP registration](/en/vasp-georgia)** — for businesses providing **virtual asset (crypto) services**. This is a **regulatory authorisation** from the National Bank, not a tax status, and sits on top of your company form.

**[Investor guarantees](/en/investor-guarantees-in-georgia)** — not a status you apply for, but the statutory protections every **foreign investor** enjoys: equal treatment with local investors, free repatriation of profits, and protection against expropriation.
