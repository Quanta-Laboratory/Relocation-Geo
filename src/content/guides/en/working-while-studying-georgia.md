International students often ask whether they can work while studying in Georgia. Since the labour migration reforms introduced in **2026**, the answer is straightforward: studying and working are now treated as separate legal rights.

## Studying and working are separate rights

A **Study Residence Permit** authorises you to study in Georgia. By itself, however, it does **not** authorise you to undertake paid employment, self-employment or entrepreneurial activity.

Since **1 March 2026**, the **Law of Georgia on Labour Migration** generally requires foreign nationals to obtain a **Right to Labour Activity** before carrying out labour activity in Georgia, unless they fall within one of the statutory exemptions.

## What students need to work

If you wish to work while studying, you will generally need to obtain a **Right to Labour Activity** in addition to your study status.

Applications are submitted through the **Labour Migration Portal**, and the standard processing period is generally up to **30 days**, although processing times may vary.

Obtaining the right to work does not replace your Study Residence Permit. Rather, it gives you the additional authorisation required to carry out labour activity while you remain in Georgia as a student.

For detailed guidance on eligibility, exemptions and the application process, see our **[Right to Labour Activity in Georgia](/en/right-to-work-georgia)** guide.

## Remote work for clients abroad

Some international students earn income remotely from clients or employers located outside Georgia.

Depending on the circumstances, certain forms of remote work performed exclusively for a **non-resident employer or client** may fall within one of the statutory exemptions from the Right to Labour Activity requirement. Whether an exemption applies depends on the specific facts and the relevant legal provisions.

If you intend to work remotely while studying, it is advisable to confirm that your circumstances fall within an applicable exemption before relying on it. The tax aspects of remote work are discussed in our **[Georgia for Digital Nomads](/en/georgia-for-digital-nomads/)** guide.

## Penalties

Carrying out labour activity without the required authorisation may result in administrative penalties. A first offence currently carries a **GEL 2,000** fine, with higher penalties applying to repeat violations.

Before accepting employment or beginning any business or self-employed activity in Georgia, ensure that you have obtained any required work authorisation.

## A note on changing rules

The Right to Labour Activity framework is relatively new, and administrative practice continues to develop. Always confirm the current legal requirements, available exemptions and processing times before starting any paid work in Georgia.
