A common question from international students is whether they can earn money on the side while studying in Georgia. The short answer changed in 2026: your student status covers studying, but working now needs a separate permission. Here is how the two fit together.

## Studying and working are separate rights

A study residence permit authorises you to study — it does not, on its own, give you the right to take paid work or run a business. This separation became sharper on **1 March 2026**, when amendments to the Law on Labour Migration introduced a mandatory work-permit system. Since then, most foreign nationals must hold a **"Right to Labour Activity"** permit before doing paid employment, self-employment or entrepreneurial activity in Georgia, regardless of any residence permit they hold.

## What students need to work

If you want to work while studying, you generally need to obtain the work permit in addition to your study status. The permit is applied for through the **Labour Migration portal**, with a review that can take up to around 30 days, after which the corresponding work status (such as a D1 visa or work residence permit) applies. In other words, working is no longer something that simply comes with being in the country — it is a separate, deliberate step. The wider rules are set out in the [guide to Georgian visas](https://relocation.ge/en/guide-to-georgian-visas/).

## Remote work for clients abroad

Many students earn online from clients outside Georgia. Work performed entirely remotely, with no Georgian employer and no local business registration, may fall outside the work-permit requirement — but officials are still clarifying exactly where this line sits. Until that is settled, treat remote freelance income cautiously and confirm your specific situation rather than assuming you are exempt. The tax side of remote work is covered in the [digital nomads guide](https://relocation.ge/en/georgia-for-digital-nomads/).

## Penalties

Working without the required permit is enforced: a first offence carries a fine of 2,000 GEL, applied to the worker and, in employment cases, to the employer, with higher penalties for repeat breaches. The practical takeaway for students is simple — sort out work authorisation before you start earning locally, not after.

## A note on changing rules

The work-permit framework is new and its details are still being implemented. Confirm the current requirements, exemptions and processing times through the Labour Migration portal before taking any paid work.
