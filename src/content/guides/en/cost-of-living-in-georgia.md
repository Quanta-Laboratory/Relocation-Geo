One of Georgia's strongest draws is affordability: housing, food and everyday services cost far less than in Western Europe or North America. Actual spending depends heavily on the city and your lifestyle, so treat the figures here as indicative planning ranges rather than fixed prices — local rents in particular move quickly.

## A rough monthly budget

As a planning guide, a single person comfortably budgets around **USD 700–1,200 per month** including rent, while a couple is often in the **USD 1,200–2,000** range. Families and those wanting central, newer housing or frequent dining out will be higher. The single biggest variable is rent, followed by how often you eat out and whether you run a car

## Rent

Rent is the largest line item and varies most by location. Central Tbilisi and Batumi command the highest prices, newer buildings cost more than Soviet-era stock, and renting a room or sharing an apartment cuts costs sharply. Prices are negotiated freely between tenant and landlord — the [renting a home guide](https://relocation.ge/en/renting-a-home-in-georgia/) explains how leases and deposits work.

## Food, transport and daily life

Groceries from local markets and supermarkets are inexpensive, and eating out — especially at Georgian restaurants and bakeries — is very affordable. City transport (metro, buses, marshrutkas) costs little, and taxis and ride-hailing are cheap by Western standards. Owning a car adds fuel, insurance and parking costs.

## Utilities and connectivity

Apartment utilities — electricity, water, gas and rubbish collection — are generally modest, though winter heating pushes bills up where it relies on electricity or gas. Home fibre internet and mobile data are inexpensive and widely available; see the [mobile and internet guide](https://relocation.ge/en/mobile-and-internet-in-georgia/).

## Money and payments

The currency is the Georgian lari (GEL). Card payments are standard in cities, ATMs are plentiful, and the banking system is modern and foreigner-friendly — opening an account is covered in the [banking guide](https://relocation.ge/en/banking-in-georgia/).

## A note on figures

Prices, and rents especially, change with demand and season. Use these ranges to plan, then verify current costs on the ground and through up-to-date local listings before budgeting precisely.
