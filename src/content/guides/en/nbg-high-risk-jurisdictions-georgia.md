**In short.** Georgia keeps its own list of "high-risk jurisdictions" for the purposes of its anti-money-laundering law. The National Bank of Georgia (NBG) approves it on the recommendation of the Financial Monitoring Service. The current version is **Order No 12/04 of the President of the NBG of 21 January 2026**, listing **33 countries and territories**. Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Turkey, Israel, India, China and the UAE are **not** on it.

## Legal basis

The Law of Georgia on Facilitating the Prevention of Money Laundering and the Financing of Terrorism (No 5226-Iს of 30 October 2019) binds "accountable persons": banks, microfinance organisations, payment service providers, currency exchanges, brokers, insurers, virtual-asset service providers, notaries, lawyers, accountants, auditors, gambling operators, real-estate agents and others.

The list itself sits in NBG Order No 240/04 of 18 December 2019, amended by Orders No 99/04 (1 May 2020), No 197/04 (10 November 2020) and No 12/04 (21 January 2026, in force on publication).

## What Article 19 says

Article 19 (as amended by Law No 4237 of 30 May 2024):

1. **Definition.** A high-risk jurisdiction is a country or territory whose AML/CFT system has serious deficiencies. The NBG approves and amends the list on the Service's recommendation.
2. **General rule.** Enhanced preventive measures, proportionate to the risk, the relationship and the transaction, apply to natural and legal persons located in a listed jurisdiction.
3. **Three triggers** for enhanced due diligence under Article 18:
   - (a) the client is a legal person registered in, or managed or operated from, a listed jurisdiction, or the Georgian branch of such a person;
   - (b) the client is a natural person whose registered and/or actual residence is in a listed jurisdiction;
   - (c) the transaction is made or completed through a financial institution located in a listed jurisdiction.
4. **Residents' exemption.** Trigger (b) is not mandatory for Georgian citizens or foreigners holding a Georgian residence permit.
5. **Group exemption.** Trigger (c) is not mandatory where that institution is a subsidiary or branch of a Georgian financial institution and group-level compliance manages the risk.
6. **Countermeasures.** The competent authority must be able to apply proportionate measures, whether or not the FATF has issued a public statement.

Article 18 sets out what enhanced due diligence can involve: more information on the client's and beneficial owner's assets and activity, more frequent updates of identification data, the purpose of the relationship and of individual transactions, senior-management approval, reasonable steps to establish the source of wealth and funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring. Article 16 also bars an accountable person from relying on a third-party introducer located, managed or operating in a listed jurisdiction.

### What changed in January 2026

Compared with Order No 197/04 of 10 November 2020 (30 jurisdictions):

- **Added (9):** Burkina Faso, Venezuela, Vietnam, Cameroon, Lebanon, Mozambique, South Africa, South Sudan, Haiti.
- **Removed (6):** Botswana, Ghana, Zimbabwe, Cambodia, Mauritius, Tajikistan.

Some press reports gave different figures (11 in, 11 out) and even listed China. That does not match the published order: China is not on the list, and Iraq and Vanuatu were already on it.

## How it differs from the FATF and EU lists

The NBG list is national. All three FATF "call for action" jurisdictions (North Korea, Iran, Myanmar) are on it, but many FATF "increased monitoring" jurisdictions — Bulgaria, Monaco, Kenya, Laos, Nepal, the British Virgin Islands — are not, while it includes jurisdictions the FATF does not list, such as Albania, Colombia, Pakistan, Panama, the Cayman Islands and Jamaica. Large Georgian banks also apply FATF, US/EU/UK sanctions and their own risk-appetite policies, so "not on the NBG list" does not mean "no extra questions".

## Russia and Belarus

Neither is on the NBG list. Bank of Georgia and Basisbank, however, state in their published risk documents that they treat Russia and Belarus as higher-risk. For clients with Russian or Belarusian passports, or money coming from Russian banks, what matters in practice is each bank's internal policy and sanctions compliance, not Article 19. See the [list of banks in Georgia](/en/list-of-banks-in-georgia).

## What to do

- **Nationals or residents of a listed country living in Georgia:** get a [residence permit](/en/guide-to-residence-permits) — it removes the mandatory residence-based trigger. Prepare source-of-funds documents anyway.
- **Owners of companies registered in listed jurisdictions** (Panama, Seychelles, Cayman, Bahamas, Gibraltar, etc.): expect enhanced due diligence even if the business is run from Georgia. A Georgian LLC or [individual entrepreneur registration](/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely) is often simpler.
- **Payments through banks in listed jurisdictions:** keep invoices, contracts and an explanation of the commercial purpose ready.
- **Property buyers:** notaries and real-estate agents are accountable persons too; expect source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership questions.

## Where to check for updates

The list can change at any time with immediate effect. Check NBG Order No 240/04 and its amending orders on matsne.gov.ge, the Financial Monitoring Service website (fms.gov.ge) and the FATF lists (fatf-gafi.org).
