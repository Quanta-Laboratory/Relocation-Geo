Georgia does not tax company profit in the way most countries do. Since **1 January 2017**, most companies have been taxed under a **distribution-based** system — widely called the **"Estonian" model** — in which **retained and reinvested profit is not taxed**, and profit tax falls due **only when profit is distributed** or treated as a deemed distribution. The standard rate is **15%**. 

## The model: tax on distribution, not on earning

Under the classic corporate income tax that Georgia used before 2017, a company calculated its taxable profit each year (gross income minus deductible expenses) and paid tax on that figure whether or not the money left the company. The **distribution model replaced that logic** for most companies from **1 January 2017**.

The change is not an exemption — it is a **deferral**. Profit is not taxed while it stays in the company; it is taxed **when it is taken out**. A company that earns profit and reinvests it in its own operations pays **no profit tax on that profit** until the day it distributes it. Because taxable profit no longer has to be computed annually from gross income and deductions, the tax attaches instead to a **short list of specific events**.

## The rate

| Taxpayer | Profit tax rate |
| --- | --- |
| Most companies (standard) | 15% |
| Commercial banks, credit unions, microfinance organisations, loan providers | 20% |

The rate is applied to the **grossed-up value** of the taxable amount, not to the net cash paid out — so the effective burden on a distribution is higher than 15% of the cash sum. The exact grossing-up formula should be checked against the Tax Code or with an adviser. Rates and the categories of taxpayer they apply to can change, so **confirm the current rate with the Revenue Service** before relying on it.

## The tax base: four taxable events

Instead of taxing annual profit, the model taxes the **grossed-up value** of four categories of transaction:

| Taxable event | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Distributed profit** | Distribution of profit to a partner as a **dividend**, in monetary or non-monetary form. |
| **Costs not related to economic activity** | Expenses that do not serve the business — for example **undocumented expenses**, spending not aimed at generating income, and interest paid above the ceiling set by the Minister of Finance. |
| **Free-of-charge supplies** | Free-of-charge delivery of **goods, services or funds**; shortages of inventory or fixed assets are also treated as free supply when identified. |
| **Over-limit representation costs** | **Representation (entertainment) expenses** exceeding the statutory limit set by the Tax Code. |

The purpose of the last three categories is to stop profit being taken out of the company in disguised forms — as fake expenses, gifts or perks — without ever being taxed as a distribution. Certain transactions with persons in **preferential (low-tax) jurisdictions**, such as loans or advances to them, are also brought into immediate taxation, and the Tax Code allows previously paid profit tax to be **credited back** in defined cases (for example when an advance or loan is returned).

Dividends distributed **between Georgian resident companies**, and dividends received from most foreign enterprises, are generally **outside** the profit tax base — an important point for holding structures, but one with exceptions for preferential-regime jurisdictions.

## Who is outside the standard model

The standard 15% distribution model covers **most companies**. Some financial-sector entities — **commercial banks, credit unions, microfinance organisations and loan providers** — are taxed at the higher **20%** rate. Whether a particular financial institution or insurer is subject to the distribution model, a different regime, or a special transition rule can depend on the entity type and the year, so **confirm the treatment of a specific company with the Revenue Service** rather than assuming the standard rules apply.

## Filing and payment

The profit tax **reporting period is the calendar month**. A company submits a **monthly profit tax return** and pays the tax, generally by the **15th day of the month following** the month in which a taxable event occurred. In months with no distribution or other taxable event, there is typically nothing to pay. Returns are filed **electronically** through the Revenue Service portal at **[rs.ge](https://rs.ge/)**. Deadlines and the exact filing obligations depend on the company's circumstances, so confirm them with the Revenue Service or an adviser.

## How this fits with the rest of the system

The distribution model is the **default** for ordinary Georgian companies. Alongside it, Georgia offers **special statuses and regimes** that change the profit-tax outcome for qualifying businesses:

- **[Special tax regimes](/en/special-tax-regimes-georgia)** — the small, micro and fixed-status regimes aimed at individual entrepreneurs and very small businesses.
- **[Business statuses](/en/business-statuses-georgia)** — including **International Company** and **Virtual Zone** status, which can substantially reduce the effective profit-tax rate for qualifying IT and service companies.
- **[Running a company in Georgia](/en/running-a-company-in-georgia)** — the practical duties of operating a Georgian company, including tax registration and reporting.

Profit tax sits separately from **[personal income tax](/en/personal-income-tax-georgia)** (which taxes individuals) and from **[VAT](/en/vat-in-georgia)** (a transaction tax that can apply regardless of profit-tax position).

This guide is general information, **not tax advice**. The taxable-event rules, the treatment of preferential-jurisdiction transactions and the position of financial institutions are detailed and fact-specific, and the figures can change, so take professional advice and confirm the current rules with the **Revenue Service** before relying on a particular treatment.
