Georgia created a dedicated **IT Residence Permit** to attract foreign software developers, IT founders and the staff of international technology companies. It is one of the most useful permits for the remote-tech crowd because it is granted for a relatively long **three years** and recognises both employees and entrepreneurs. This guide explains who qualifies, the income and experience tests, the all-important absence rule and how to apply — checked against the **Law on the Legal Status of Aliens and Stateless Persons** and the procedures in **Government Resolution No. 520**.

## Validity: three years, up to twelve

The IT Residence Permit is issued for **three years** and can be **renewed in three-year steps, up to a maximum of twelve years**. That makes it markedly more durable than the standard six-to-twelve-month work or study permits, and it is one of the reasons IT professionals favour it. After the twelve-year ceiling, a holder who meets the conditions can move onto a **[permanent residence permit](https://relocation.ge/en/permanent-residence-permit-georgia/)** or another long-term basis.

## Who qualifies: three categories

The permit is open to three groups, plus their family members:

**IT employees** — foreigners employed in IT whose engagement is recorded in the **Ministry of Labour's labour-migration database**. This ties the IT permit to Georgia's wider [right-to-work system](https://relocation.ge/en/right-to-work-georgia/).

**IT entrepreneurs** — foreigners registered as an **[Individual Entrepreneur](https://relocation.ge/en/register-individual-entrepreneur-remotely/)** with **Small Business Status** whose economic activity is in the IT field.

**Company representatives** — executives or representatives of **international IT companies** operating in Georgia.

## The income and experience tests

Applicants must demonstrate **at least two years** of IT-related work or economic activity, and **annual income of at least USD 25,000** in Georgian lari equivalent.

A practical detail trips people up: the income should arrive in **at least two separate payments, spaced at least 30 days apart**, rather than as a single transfer. This is meant to evidence ongoing activity rather than a one-off deposit, so plan your invoicing and banking accordingly.

## The absence rule

The IT permit is built around **real residence**. It is **terminated if the holder is absent from Georgia for 183 days or more in any 12-month period**, absent an applicable exception. If your work involves long stretches abroad, keep a careful record of your days in and out of the country — the same discipline that matters for [tax residency](https://relocation.ge/en/tax-residency-georgia/).

## Fees and timing

The standard application review takes **90 days for a fee of GEL 500**; an **expedited 60-day** review costs **GEL 750**. If approved, you collect a **residence (ID) card**, which carries its own fee depending on the speed of issue (from GEL 60 for ten working days up to GEL 150 for same-day). Because fee schedules are revised from time to time, confirm the current figures with the **Public Service Development Agency** before applying.

## How to apply

Confirm which of the three categories fits you and gather the evidence it requires — employment and labour-migration registration, or IE registration with Small Business Status, or proof of your role with an international IT company — together with proof of two years' experience and the income record. File the application (online, in person or through a representative) **before any current permit expires**, and respond promptly to any request for further documents during the review.

This guide is general information, **not legal advice**. Eligibility classifications and fees can change, so confirm the current position with the **Public Service Development Agency** or an adviser before acting.
