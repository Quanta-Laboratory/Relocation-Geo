Each year resident individuals in Georgia may have to **declare and pay property tax**. For 2026 the key change is the **income threshold**: from **1 October 2026** a resident family is exempt from property tax on **taxable property other than land** where its income in the **previous calendar year did not exceed GEL 100,000** — up from the old GEL 40,000 — and the former 0.05–0.2% middle band was abolished (**Tax Code, article 206, paragraph 1, subparagraph "a"**).

This guide explains **who must declare, how the income test works, how Small Business status changes it, and the deadlines**. It is **general information, not legal or tax advice** — confirm the current wording and your own position with the **[Revenue Service](https://www.rs.ge/)** before relying on it. To estimate figures, see the **[property tax calculator](/en/georgia-property-tax-calculator)**; for the wider framework, see **[Property Tax in Georgia](/en/property-tax-georgia)**.

## Who has to declare in 2026

Property tax is a **local tax** under the Tax Code. For an individual, the duty to declare and pay depends on **household income**, not on the value of the property alone.

- If the family's income in the **previous calendar year did not exceed GEL 100,000**, the individual's **taxable property other than land is exempt** (art. 206.1.a), and as a rule **no declaration** is required for that property.
- If the family's income **exceeded GEL 100,000**, property tax is due at **0.8%–1%** of the market value of the taxable property (the exact rate within that band is set by the municipality), and a **declaration must be filed**.

**Land is separate.** The income exemption does **not** cover land: land tax can be due even when the rest of your property is exempt. Owners are liable for land they hold or use as at **1 April** of the tax year.

## What counts as taxable property

For an individual, taxable property includes:

- **real estate** — flats, houses, commercial premises, buildings or parts of them, including **unfinished construction**;
- **light cars** classified under **HS code 8703** of the foreign-trade nomenclature;
- **yachts and motorboats (cutters)**;
- **aeroplanes and helicopters**.

Motorcycles and vehicles outside code 8703 are **not** objects of this tax. Taxable property is valued at **market value as at 31 December** of the previous year, and tax is calculated **in proportion to the period of ownership** during the year.

## The GEL 100,000 income test

The income used for the threshold is the **family's income in the previous calendar year**, and it is **broader than salary**. It includes, in principle, all income and benefits — **gross (accrued) salary, income from economic activity, rental income, interest and other income** — even where that income is exempt for income-tax purposes, unless the Tax Code specifically excludes it (the family-income definition for property tax sits in **Tax Code article 202**).

"Family" for this purpose means the person, their spouse, minor children and stepchildren, and — where they live permanently with the taxpayer and share a common household — parents, children, stepchildren, siblings, grandparents and grandchildren. **The taxpayer** decides which of those relatives are part of the household.

### Income that is excluded

Some income is **left out** of the family-income test, including:

- property received from **family members by inheritance, gift or on divorce**;
- income from the **sale of a home** the person owned for **more than 2 years**;
- income under the **Micro Business** and **fixed-tax** regimes.

Because these exclusions are specific, do not assume that every income-tax-exempt amount is ignored for property tax.

## Individual entrepreneurs and Small Business (1%) status

This is the point that most often trips up **individual entrepreneurs**. For the **property-tax income test**, a person with **[Small Business status](/en/small-business-status-georgia)** counts **only 25%** of the income taxed under the **1% regime**, together with any income **outside** that regime in full. This rule is part of the family-income definition in **Tax Code article 202** (the special-regime inclusion, parts 6–7).

In practice this means a sole trader whose **only** income is 1%-regime turnover reaches the GEL 100,000 threshold only once turnover is about **four times** higher:

> **GEL 400,000** of 1% turnover × **25%** = **GEL 100,000** counted.

So, with no other income:

- 1% turnover **up to about GEL 400,000** → counted income **≤ GEL 100,000** → taxable property (other than land) **exempt**, and generally **no property-tax declaration**;
- 1% turnover **above about GEL 400,000** → counted income **over GEL 100,000** → property tax **due** and a **declaration required**.

If the entrepreneur also has income **outside** the 1% regime — salary, rent, non-regime business income — that part counts **in full** and is **added** to the 25% figure, so the threshold can be reached at a lower turnover.

This is only the **property-tax** income rule. It does **not** change how the **1% Small Business tax** itself is worked out — see **[Small Business status](/en/small-business-status-georgia)** for the turnover-tax rules.

## Deadlines and filing

- **File** the annual property-tax declaration (covering both taxable property and land) with the **Revenue Service by 1 November**.
- **Pay** the property tax and land tax by **15 November**.
- A **family** with more than one owner may file a **single joint declaration**.
- If **no liability** arises but you **filed a declaration in the previous year**, you must **notify** the Revenue Service **by 1 November** that you will not file for the current year, in the prescribed form.

Where a declaration was filed (or the Revenue Service assessed the tax) in the previous year and nothing material has changed, the Revenue Service can assess the current year's tax automatically on the previous data — but a purchase, sale, change in value, change in family income or new land can make a fresh or amended declaration necessary.
